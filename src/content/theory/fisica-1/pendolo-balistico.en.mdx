<Layer kind="intuizione" title="The idea in brief">

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-balistico/idea">

The ballistic pendulum is the perfect example of 'each phase has its law': the collision is violent and very fast → momentum rules; the rise is gentle and dissipation-free → energy rules. Mixing up the two regimes leads to gross errors, and that is exactly what the examiner wants to check you can tell apart.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-balistico/definizioni">

## Definitions

The ballistic pendulum is a device for measuring the speed of a projectile: a block of mass M hangs from strings; the projectile of mass m embeds itself in it (perfectly inelastic collision) and the system rises by a height h. The problem is solved in TWO phases with different conservation laws: in the collision momentum is conserved (not energy), in the rise mechanical energy is conserved (not momentum, because gravity and tensions act).

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-balistico/derivazione" class="derivation">

## Step-by-step derivation

<p class="muted">Try deriving it aloud first, then open one step at a time.</p>

<details id="passo-1">
<summary>Step 1 · Phase 1 — perfectly inelastic collision: conserve P</summary>

$$
mv_0 = (m + M)V \;\Rightarrow\; V = \frac{m}{m+M}v_0
$$

During the very short collision the external forces (weight, tension) give negligible impulse: P is conserved. Energy is NOT: the projectile embeds itself, deforming the block.

</details>

<details id="passo-2">
<summary>Step 2 · Energy dissipated in the collision</summary>

$$
\Delta K = \frac{1}{2}(m+M)V^2 - \frac{1}{2}mv_0^2 = -\frac{M}{m+M}\cdot\frac{1}{2}mv_0^2
$$

The fraction of energy lost is M/(m+M): with a light projectile and a heavy block almost all the initial energy ends up in deformation and heat.

</details>

<details id="passo-3">
<summary>Step 3 · Phase 2 — rise: conserve mechanical energy</summary>

$$
\frac{1}{2}(m+M)V^2 = (m+M)gh \;\Rightarrow\; V = \sqrt{2gh}
$$

After the collision the only forces are weight (conservative) and the tensions (perpendicular to the motion, no work): E is conserved up to the highest point, where the system is momentarily at rest.

</details>

<details id="passo-4">
<summary>Step 4 · Combine the two phases</summary>

$$
v_0 = \frac{m+M}{m}\sqrt{2gh}
$$

By measuring m, M and h (or the swing angle: h = L(1−cos θ)) one obtains the speed of the projectile: a difficult measurement reduced to three easy ones.

</details>

<details id="passo-5">
<summary>Step 5 · Height as a function of the maximum angle</summary>

$$
h = L(1 - \cos\theta_{max}) \;\Rightarrow\; v_0 = \frac{m+M}{m}\sqrt{2gL(1-\cos\theta_{max})}
$$

Practical form: in experiments one reads the angle of maximum swing.

</details>

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-balistico/ipotesi">

## Validity assumptions

Collision much shorter than the period of the pendulum (so P is conserved and the pendulum does not move during the collision); projectile stays embedded; inextensible massless strings; negligible friction during the rise.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-balistico/casi-limite">

## Limiting cases

m ≪ M: V ≈ (m/M)v₀ very small, almost all the energy dissipated (the real case). m ≫ M: V ≈ v₀, almost nothing is dissipated. h → 0: v₀ → 0, consistent.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio" title="Worked example">

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-balistico/esempio">

A 10 g bullet embeds itself in a 2.0 kg wooden block hanging from 1.5 m strings; the pendulum rises to a maximum angle of 18°. Find: (a) the rise height; (b) the speed of the block right after the collision; (c) the speed of the bullet; (d) the fraction of energy dissipated.

1. **Rise.** $h = L(1 - \cos 18°) \approx 1.5 \cdot 0.04894 \approx 0.0734\ \text{m}$; $V = \sqrt{2gh} \approx 1.20\ \text{m/s}$.
2. **Collision.** $v_0 = \dfrac{m+M}{m}V = 201 \cdot 1.20 \approx 241\ \text{m/s}$.
3. **Energy.** Dissipated fraction $M/(m+M) = 2.0/2.01 \approx 0.995$: 99.5% of the bullet's energy becomes deformation and heat.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori" title="Common mistakes">

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-balistico/errori">

- Conserving energy across the collision: ½mv₀² = (m+M)gh is WRONG and overestimates h.
- Conserving momentum during the rise (non-negligible external forces act over long times).
- Forgetting the factor (m+M)/m when going back from V to v₀.
- Using h = L sin θ instead of h = L(1 − cos θ).

</div>
