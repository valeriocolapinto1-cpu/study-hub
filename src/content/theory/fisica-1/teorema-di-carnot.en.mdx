<Layer kind="intuizione" title="The idea in brief">

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-carnot/idea">

The theorem puts a universal ceiling on efficiency: however ingenious the design, efficiency is limited by the temperature difference alone. The trick of the proof is elegant: if someone built an engine better than Carnot's, coupling it to a reversed Carnot engine would give a free heat pump — and nature, by the second law, gives no free lunches.

</div>

<Layer kind="formalismo" title="Definitions and derivation">

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-carnot/definizioni">

## Definitions

Carnot's theorem states: (1) no heat engine working between two reservoirs can have an efficiency greater than the reversible Carnot engine working between the same reservoirs; (2) all reversible engines between the same two reservoirs have the same efficiency, $\eta_{rev} = 1 - T_F/T_C$, independent of the fluid and the mechanism. The theorem founds the absolute thermodynamic temperature scale.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-carnot/derivazione" class="derivation">

## Step-by-step derivation

<p class="muted">Try deriving it aloud first, then open one step at a time.</p>

<details id="passo-1">
<summary>Step 1 · Setting up a proof by contradiction</summary>

$$
\text{Assumption: } \eta_X > \eta_{rev}
$$

Suppose there exists an engine X (of any kind) more efficient than the reversible engine R between the same reservoirs T_C and T_F.

</details>

<details id="passo-2">
<summary>Step 2 · Reverse the reversible engine</summary>

$$
R \to R^{-1}: \;\text{absorbs } W,\; \text{takes } Q_F,\; \text{releases } Q_C
$$

R is reversible: I can run it as a refrigerator with the same exchanges with opposite signs. I drive it with the work produced by X.

</details>

<details id="passo-3">
<summary>Step 3 · Balance of the composite system X + R⁻¹</summary>

$$
\eta_X > \eta_R \Rightarrow \frac{W}{Q_C^X} > \frac{W}{Q_C^R} \Rightarrow Q_C^X < Q_C^R
$$

Size R⁻¹ so that it uses exactly the work W produced by X. For the same W, the 'better' engine takes less heat from the hot reservoir than R⁻¹ returns to it.

</details>

<details id="passo-4">
<summary>Step 4 · Contradiction with Clausius</summary>

$$
Q_{net,\,C} = Q_C^R - Q_C^X > 0 \;\text{flows into the hot reservoir}, \qquad W_{net} = 0
$$

The composite system, cyclic and with no net external work, transfers heat from the cold reservoir to the hot one as its only result: it violates the Clausius statement. Hence η_X ≤ η_rev.

</details>

<details id="passo-5">
<summary>Step 5 · Second part: all reversible engines are equivalent</summary>

$$
\eta_{R_1} \le \eta_{R_2} \;\text{and}\; \eta_{R_2} \le \eta_{R_1} \;\Rightarrow\; \eta_{R_1} = \eta_{R_2}
$$

If R₁ and R₂ are both reversible, apply the argument both ways (each can act as the refrigerator for the other): the efficiencies must coincide. Since for the ideal gas η = 1 − T_F/T_C, this is the efficiency of EVERY reversible engine.

</details>

<details id="passo-6">
<summary>Step 6 · Consequence: absolute thermodynamic temperature</summary>

$$
\frac{|Q_F|}{Q_C} = \frac{T_F}{T_C}
$$

The ratio of the heats exchanged by a reversible engine depends only on the temperatures: the temperature scale can be DEFINED through heats, without reference to any substance. The scale coincides with the ideal-gas one.

</details>

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-carnot/ipotesi">

## Validity assumptions

Validity of the second law (Clausius or Kelvin–Planck statement); two ideal thermal reservoirs at fixed temperatures; cyclic machines. Engine X is completely arbitrary (real, irreversible, any fluid).

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-carnot/casi-limite">

## Limiting cases

Irreversible engine: η &lt; η_rev strictly (friction, finite temperature jumps). Reversible engine: η = 1 − T_F/T_C exactly. The theorem says nothing about power: the ideal Carnot engine is infinitely slow.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio" title="Worked example">

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-carnot/esempio">

An inventor claims an engine that, working between 500 K and 300 K, absorbs 10 kJ per cycle and produces 4.5 kJ of work. Find: (a) the claimed efficiency; (b) the maximum theoretical efficiency; (c) the maximum work per cycle for the same absorbed heat. Is the claim credible?

1. **Comparison with Carnot.** $\eta_{claim} = 4.5/10 = 0.45$; $\eta_{rev} = 1 - 300/500 = 0.40$. The maximum work is $0.40 \cdot 10 = 4.0\ \text{kJ}$.
2. **Verdict.** The claimed efficiency exceeds Carnot's: by Carnot's theorem the engine is impossible (it would violate the second law).

</div>

<Layer kind="errori" title="Common mistakes">

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-carnot/errori">

- Proving the theorem using only the ideal-gas Carnot cycle: the strength of the theorem is its universality, which requires the argument by contradiction.
- Forgetting that reversing requires R to be reversible: a real engine cannot be 'run backwards' with the same exchanges.
- Concluding that all engines have η = 1 − T_F/T_C: only reversible ones; the others fall below.
- Neglecting the clause 'same two reservoirs'.

</div>
