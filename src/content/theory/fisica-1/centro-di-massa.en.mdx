<Layer kind="intuizione" title="The idea in brief">

<div data-note-id="teoria/centro-di-massa/idea">

The centre of mass is the system's 'spokesperson': however the pieces interact, pulling and pushing each other, no internal force can move the group's centre — that takes a push from outside. This is why you cannot lift yourself by pulling your own hair, and why fireworks burst 'around' the trajectory the rocket would have followed.

</div>

<Layer kind="formalismo" title="Definitions and derivation">

<div data-note-id="teoria/centro-di-massa/definizioni">

## Definitions

For a system of N point particles, the centre of mass (CM) is the point with position $\vec{r}_{cm} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_i m_i\vec{r}_i$, with $M = \sum_i m_i$ (for a continuous body the integral $\vec{r}_{cm} = \frac{1}{M}\int \vec{r}\,dm$). The first cardinal equation states that the resultant of the external forces alone governs the motion of the centre of mass: $\vec{F}^{ext} = M\vec{a}_{cm}$. Equivalently, $\vec{F}^{ext} = d\vec{P}/dt$ with $\vec{P} = M\vec{v}_{cm}$ the total momentum.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/centro-di-massa/derivazione" class="derivation">

## Step-by-step derivation

<p class="muted">Try deriving it aloud first, then open one step at a time.</p>

<details id="passo-1">
<summary>Step 1 · Newton for each point of the system</summary>

$$
m_i\vec{a}_i = \vec{F}_i^{ext} + \sum_{j\neq i}\vec{f}_{ij}
$$

Each point is acted upon by external forces and by internal forces f_ij exerted by the other points of the system.

</details>

<details id="passo-2">
<summary>Step 2 · Sum over all points</summary>

$$
\sum_i m_i\vec{a}_i = \sum_i\vec{F}_i^{ext} + \sum_i\sum_{j\neq i}\vec{f}_{ij}
$$

The double sum contains each pair twice: f_ij and f_ji.

</details>

<details id="passo-3">
<summary>Step 3 · Internal forces cancel (third law)</summary>

$$
\vec{f}_{ij} + \vec{f}_{ji} = \vec{0} \;\Rightarrow\; \sum_i\sum_{j\neq i}\vec{f}_{ij} = \vec{0}
$$

Action and reaction are opposite: the sum of all internal forces is identically zero, whatever their nature.

</details>

<details id="passo-4">
<summary>Step 4 · Recognise the acceleration of the centre of mass</summary>

$$
\sum_i m_i\vec{a}_i = \frac{d^2}{dt^2}\sum_i m_i\vec{r}_i = M\ddot{\vec{r}}_{cm} = M\vec{a}_{cm}
$$

By definition of r_cm: Σmᵢrᵢ = M r_cm, and differentiate twice.

</details>

<details id="passo-5">
<summary>Step 5 · First cardinal equation</summary>

$$
\vec{F}^{ext} = M\vec{a}_{cm} = \frac{d\vec{P}}{dt}, \qquad \vec{P} = \sum_i m_i \vec{v}_i = M\vec{v}_{cm}
$$

The CM moves like a point particle of mass M subject only to the external resultant: internal forces cannot move the centre of mass.

</details>

<details id="passo-6">
<summary>Step 6 · Conservation of momentum</summary>

$$
\vec{F}^{ext} = \vec{0} \;\Rightarrow\; \vec{P} = \text{const} \;\Rightarrow\; \vec{v}_{cm} = \text{const}
$$

Isolated system: the CM goes on in uniform rectilinear motion whatever happens inside (explosions, collisions, springs).

</details>

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/centro-di-massa/ipotesi">

## Validity assumptions

Validity of Newton's third law for internal forces; constant masses; inertial reference frame. No rigidity assumption is needed: it holds for any system.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/centro-di-massa/casi-limite">

## Limiting cases

Isolated system: P constant (the basis for studying collisions). A projectile exploding in flight: the fragments scatter but their CM continues along the original parabola. Rigid body: r_cm coincides with the geometric centre if density is uniform and there is symmetry.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio" title="Worked example">

<div data-note-id="teoria/centro-di-massa/esempio">

A man of mass m = 75 kg stands at the stern of a boat of mass M = 150 kg and length 4.5 m, at rest in calm water (negligible friction). The man walks to the bow. Find: (a) the displacement of the boat; (b) the displacement of the man relative to the shore; (c) the velocity of the boat when the man walks at 1.2 m/s relative to the shore.

1. **The CM stays still.** No external horizontal force: $m\Delta x_m + M\Delta x_b = 0$. The relative displacement is L: $\Delta x_m - \Delta x_b = 4.5\ \text{m}$.
2. **Displacements.** $\Delta x_b = -mL/(m+M) = -1.5\ \text{m}$ (the boat moves back), $\Delta x_m = ML/(m+M) = 3.0\ \text{m}$.
3. **Velocity.** Zero total P: $v_b = -m v_m/M = -0.60\ \text{m/s}$.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori" title="Common mistakes">

<div data-note-id="teoria/centro-di-massa/errori">

- Including internal forces in the first cardinal equation: only external ones count.
- Believing that the CM must lie inside the body (counterexamples: ring, boomerang).
- Confusing v_cm with the velocity of a specific point of the body.
- Using conservation of P when the external resultant is not zero (e.g. during a collision with impulsive external constraints).

</div>
