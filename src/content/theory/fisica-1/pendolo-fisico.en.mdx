<Layer kind="intuizione" title="The idea in brief">

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-fisico/idea">

In the physical pendulum the weight 'pulls down' the centre of mass with lever arm d, but it must rotate the whole inertia I distributed in the body. The ratio I/(Md) condenses the competition: more inertia far from the axis slows the oscillation, a longer lever arm of the weight speeds it up. The reduced length translates everything into the familiar language of the simple pendulum.

</div>

<Layer kind="formalismo" title="Definitions and derivation">

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-fisico/definizioni">

## Definitions

The physical pendulum is any rigid body free to swing in a vertical plane about a fixed horizontal axis O that does not pass through the centre of mass. With d the distance between the axis and the CM and I the moment of inertia about the axis, for small oscillations the motion is harmonic with $T = 2\pi\sqrt{I/(Mgd)}$. The reduced length $\ell = I/(Md)$ is the length of the equivalent simple pendulum.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-fisico/derivazione" class="derivation">

## Step-by-step derivation

<p class="muted">Try deriving it aloud first, then open one step at a time.</p>

<details id="passo-1">
<summary>Step 1 · Second cardinal equation about the suspension axis</summary>

$$
I\ddot{\theta} = \tau_z = -Mgd\sin\theta
$$

The only torque is that of the weight, applied at the CM at distance d from the axis, with lever arm d sin θ; the pivot reaction has no torque about O. The minus sign: restoring torque.

</details>

<details id="passo-2">
<summary>Step 2 · Equation of the physical pendulum</summary>

$$
\ddot{\theta} + \frac{Mgd}{I}\sin\theta = 0
$$

Same structure as the simple pendulum with g/L → Mgd/I.

</details>

<details id="passo-3">
<summary>Step 3 · Small oscillations</summary>

$$
\sin\theta \approx \theta \;\Rightarrow\; \ddot{\theta} + \omega^2\theta = 0, \qquad \omega = \sqrt{\frac{Mgd}{I}}
$$

Harmonic motion in θ: the same linearisation as for the simple pendulum.

</details>

<details id="passo-4">
<summary>Step 4 · Period and reduced length</summary>

$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{Mgd}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{g}}, \qquad \ell = \frac{I}{Md}
$$

The physical pendulum swings like a simple pendulum of length ℓ. With Huygens–Steiner: ℓ = (I_cm + Md²)/(Md) = I_cm/(Md) + d ≥ d: the reduced length always exceeds the distance from the CM.

</details>

<details id="passo-5">
<summary>Step 5 · Consistency check: the point particle</summary>

$$
I = Md^2 \;\Rightarrow\; T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{Md^2}{Mgd}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{d}{g}}
$$

If all the mass is concentrated at distance d the simple pendulum is recovered: the general case contains the special one.

</details>

<details id="passo-6">
<summary>Step 6 · Example: uniform rod hung from one end</summary>

$$
I = \frac{1}{3}ML^2,\; d = \frac{L}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}
$$

Reduced length ℓ = 2L/3: the rod swings like a simple pendulum 2/3 of its length.

</details>

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-fisico/ipotesi">

## Validity assumptions

Rigid body; fixed frictionless horizontal axis; uniform gravitational field; for isochronism, small oscillations.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-fisico/casi-limite">

## Limiting cases

d → 0 (suspension at the CM): the restoring torque vanishes, T → ∞, neutral equilibrium. All the mass at distance d: simple pendulum. There is a d that minimises T: d = √(I_cm/M) (centroidal radius of gyration).

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio" title="Worked example">

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-fisico/esempio">

A uniform rod 0.90 m long swings about a horizontal axis through one of its ends. Find: (a) the reduced length; (b) the small-oscillation period; (c) the period if the axis instead passes 0.15 m from the centre.

1. **End.** $\ell = I/(Md) = \tfrac13 L^2/(L/2) = \tfrac23 L = 0.60\ \text{m}$, so $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\ell/g} \approx 1.554\ \text{s}$.
2. **Axis 0.15 m from the centre.** $\ell = \dfrac{L^2/12}{d} + d = \dfrac{0.0675}{0.15} + 0.15 = 0.60\ \text{m}$: same period, 1.554 s! It is the conjugate point of the end (distance between the two axes = ℓ).

</div>

<Layer kind="errori" title="Common mistakes">

<div data-note-id="teoria/pendolo-fisico/errori">

- Using I_cm instead of I about the suspension axis (Md² is missing).
- Confusing d (axis–CM distance) with the length of the body.
- Applying the simple-pendulum formula T = 2π√(L/g) with L the geometric length of the body.
- Forgetting that the period formula holds only for small oscillations.

</div>
