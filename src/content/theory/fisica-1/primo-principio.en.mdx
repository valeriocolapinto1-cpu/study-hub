<Layer kind="intuizione" title="The idea in brief">

<div data-note-id="teoria/primo-principio/idea">

Internal energy is the system's bank account; heat and work are just the two counters through which energy comes in and goes out. The balance does not remember which counter the money went through: that is why U is a state function while Q and W are not. The first law is exact bookkeeping: no energy is created or lost, it moves.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/primo-principio/definizioni">

## Definitions

The first law of thermodynamics is the conservation of energy extended to thermal phenomena: the change in internal energy of a system is $\Delta U = Q - W$, where Q is the heat absorbed BY the system and W the work done BY the system (the usual convention in Physics 1). U is a state function (it depends only on the state of the system), while Q and W depend on the transformation: in infinitesimal form $dU = \delta Q - \delta W$, with δ reminding us that heat and work are not exact differentials.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/primo-principio/derivazione" class="derivation">

## Step-by-step derivation

<p class="muted">Try deriving it aloud first, then open one step at a time.</p>

<details id="passo-1">
<summary>Step 1 · Work of quasi-static expansion</summary>

$$
\delta W = F\,dx = pA\,dx = p\,dV \;\Rightarrow\; W = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} p\,dV
$$

A gas pushing a piston of area A does work p dV. Graphically W is the area under the curve in the p-V plane: it depends on the path, not only on the end points.

</details>

<details id="passo-2">
<summary>Step 2 · U is a state function (experimental foundation)</summary>

$$
W_{adiabatic} = -\Delta U \;\;\text{independent of the path}
$$

Joule's experiments: taking a system adiabatically from state A to state B always requires the same work, however it is done (paddle wheel, resistor…). This allows U to be DEFINED.

</details>

<details id="passo-3">
<summary>Step 3 · Operational definition of heat</summary>

$$
Q \equiv \Delta U + W
$$

In a non-adiabatic transformation the balance W = −ΔU fails: heat is by definition the energy exchanged by non-mechanical means (temperature difference). The first law becomes: energy is conserved counting BOTH channels.

</details>

<details id="passo-4">
<summary>Step 4 · Application to the transformations of the ideal gas</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned} &\text{isochoric: } W = 0,\; Q = \Delta U = nc_V\Delta T \\ &\text{isobaric: } W = p\Delta V = nR\Delta T,\; Q = nc_p\Delta T \\ &\text{isothermal: } \Delta U = 0,\; Q = W = nRT\ln\frac{V_2}{V_1} \\ &\text{adiabatic: } Q = 0,\; W = -\Delta U = nc_V(T_1 - T_2) \end{aligned}
$$

The fundamental quartet. For the isotherm: W = ∫nRT dV/V = nRT ln(V₂/V₁). Each line follows from the first law plus the equation of state.

</details>

<details id="passo-5">
<summary>Step 5 · Derivation of the adiabatic equation</summary>

$$
0 = nc_V\,dT + p\,dV \;\xrightarrow{pV = nRT}\; \frac{dT}{T} = -\frac{R}{c_V}\frac{dV}{V} \;\Rightarrow\; TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}, \quad pV^{\gamma} = \text{const}
$$

From δQ = 0: dU = −pdV; eliminate T with the equation of state and integrate (R/c_V = γ−1). Adiabatics in the p-V plane are steeper than isotherms by a factor γ.

</details>

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/primo-principio/ipotesi">

## Validity assumptions

Closed system (no exchange of matter); for W = ∫p dV quasi-static transformations (p defined at every instant); sign convention stated explicitly (Q &gt; 0 absorbed, W &gt; 0 done by the system).

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/primo-principio/casi-limite">

## Limiting cases

Cyclic transformation: ΔU = 0 ⇒ Q = W (all the net heat becomes net work and vice versa). Adiabatic: Q = 0. Free expansion (into vacuum): W = 0 and Q = 0 ⇒ ΔU = 0, but it is NOT quasi-static: no W = ∫p dV.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio" title="Worked example">

<div data-note-id="teoria/primo-principio/esempio">

1.0 mol of monatomic ideal gas at 300 K and 2.0·10⁵ Pa expands until its volume doubles. Find the work done by the gas and the final temperature: (a) if the expansion is reversible isothermal; (b) if it is reversible adiabatic.

1. **Isothermal.** $W = nRT\ln 2 = 8.314 \cdot 300 \cdot 0.693 \approx 1729\ \text{J}$, T stays at 300 K, Q = W.
2. **Adiabatic.** $TV^{\gamma-1} = \text{const}$ with γ = 5/3: $T_2 = 300 \cdot 2^{-2/3} \approx 189\ \text{K}$; $W = nc_V(T_1 - T_2) = 1.5 \cdot 8.314 \cdot 111.0 \approx 1384\ \text{J}$, less than for the isotherm.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori" title="Common mistakes">

<div data-note-id="teoria/primo-principio/errori">

- Confusing sign conventions (ΔU = Q − W vs Q + W depending on how W is defined).
- Treating Q and W as state functions: 'the heat contained in a body' does not exist.
- Using W = pΔV in non-isobaric transformations.
- Applying W = ∫p dV to free expansion, which is not quasi-static.
- Using c_p to compute ΔU.

</div>
