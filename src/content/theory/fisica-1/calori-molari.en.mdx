<Layer kind="intuizione" title="The idea in brief">

<div data-note-id="teoria/calori-molari/idea">

Heating at constant volume 'pours' energy only into the internal tank; heating at constant pressure also forces you to pay the toll of expansion work, always equal to R per mole per degree: that is Mayer. In solids every atom, a spring among springs, has six pockets of ½k_BT: that is why very different materials share the same molar heat capacity 3R.

</div>

<Layer kind="formalismo" title="Definitions and derivation">

<div data-note-id="teoria/calori-molari/definizioni">

## Definitions

The molar heat capacity is the heat needed to raise the temperature of one mole by 1 K: $c = \frac{1}{n}\frac{\delta Q}{dT}$. It depends on the transformation: at constant volume c_V, at constant pressure c_p. For ideal gases Mayer's relation $c_p = c_V + R$ holds and one defines $\gamma = c_p/c_V$. For crystalline solids the Dulong–Petit law predicts c ≈ 3R, independent of the material.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/calori-molari/derivazione" class="derivation">

## Step-by-step derivation

<p class="muted">Try deriving it aloud first, then open one step at a time.</p>

<details id="passo-1">
<summary>Step 1 · Molar heat capacity at constant volume</summary>

$$
V = \text{const} \Rightarrow W = 0 \Rightarrow \delta Q = dU \;\Rightarrow\; c_V = \frac{1}{n}\Big(\frac{dU}{dT}\Big) = \frac{f}{2}R
$$

With no work, all the heat goes into internal energy: c_V directly measures the capacity of the gas to store energy in its degrees of freedom.

</details>

<details id="passo-2">
<summary>Step 2 · Molar heat capacity at constant pressure</summary>

$$
\delta Q = dU + p\,dV = nc_V\,dT + p\,dV
$$

At constant pressure the gas expands as it heats up: part of the heat pays for the expansion work.

</details>

<details id="passo-3">
<summary>Step 3 · Use the equation of state for the work term</summary>

$$
pV = nRT \;\xrightarrow{p\;\text{const}}\; p\,dV = nR\,dT
$$

Differentiate the equation of state at constant p.

</details>

<details id="passo-4">
<summary>Step 4 · Mayer's relation</summary>

$$
nc_p\,dT = nc_V\,dT + nR\,dT \;\Rightarrow\; c_p = c_V + R
$$

c_p always exceeds c_V by exactly R: the surcharge is the expansion work per degree of heating. It holds for every ideal gas, whatever f.

</details>

<details id="passo-5">
<summary>Step 5 · Numerical values and the ratio γ</summary>

$$
\text{mono: } c_V = \tfrac{3}{2}R,\; c_p = \tfrac{5}{2}R,\; \gamma = \tfrac{5}{3}; \qquad \text{diat: } c_V = \tfrac{5}{2}R,\; c_p = \tfrac{7}{2}R,\; \gamma = \tfrac{7}{5}
$$

γ = 1 + 2/f is measurable (speed of sound, adiabatics): an experimental confirmation of the count of degrees of freedom.

</details>

<details id="passo-6">
<summary>Step 6 · Ideal solid: the Dulong–Petit law</summary>

$$
\text{3 oscillators} \times 2\;\text{quadratic terms} = 6 \;\Rightarrow\; U = 3nRT \;\Rightarrow\; c \approx 3R \approx 25\;\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{mol·K}}
$$

Every atom of the lattice is a 3D oscillator: 3 kinetic + 3 elastic potential terms, each ½k_BT. The molar heat capacity is the same for (almost) all solids at room temperature; it collapses at low T because of quantum effects (Einstein, Debye).

</details>

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/calori-molari/ipotesi">

## Validity assumptions

Ideal gas at equilibrium; quasi-static transformations; degrees of freedom classically active. For Dulong–Petit: harmonic crystalline solid at high enough temperature (T above the Debye temperature).

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/calori-molari/casi-limite">

## Limiting cases

For solids at low T: c → 0 as T³ (Debye), Dulong–Petit fails. For gases at increasing T: c_V rises in steps as rotations and vibrations switch on. For liquids there is no equally simple theory.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio" title="Worked example">

<div data-note-id="teoria/calori-molari/esempio">

3.0 mol of air (ideal diatomic gas) are to be heated from 290 K to 340 K. Find the heat needed: (a) at constant volume; (b) at constant pressure; (c) the work done by the gas in case (b).

1. **Constant volume.** $Q_V = nc_V\Delta T = 3 \cdot 2.5 \cdot 8.314 \cdot 50 \approx 3118\ \text{J}$.
2. **Constant pressure.** $Q_p = nc_p\Delta T = 3 \cdot 3.5 \cdot 8.314 \cdot 50 \approx 4365\ \text{J}$; the difference is $W = nR\Delta T \approx 1247\ \text{J}$ (Mayer).

</div>

<Layer kind="errori" title="Common mistakes">

<div data-note-id="teoria/calori-molari/errori">

- Saying that the molar heat capacity is a single property of the gas: it depends on the transformation (c_V ≠ c_p, and every polytropic has its own).
- Using c_p in ΔU = nc ΔT: internal energy always goes with c_V.
- Forgetting that Mayer's relation holds only for ideal gases.
- Counting 3 degrees of freedom for the atom of a solid instead of 6 (the elastic potential is missing).

</div>
