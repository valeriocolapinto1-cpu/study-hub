<Layer kind="intuizione" title="The idea in brief">

<div data-note-id="teoria/energia-corpo-rigido/idea">

A rigid body stores kinetic energy in two 'tanks': the overall motion of the centre of mass and the rotation about it. The choice between the two equivalent forms (fixed axis vs König) is only a matter of convenience: the energy is the same. Potential energy, instead, has a single tank: the height of the centre of mass.

</div>

<Layer kind="formalismo" title="Definitions and derivation">

<div data-note-id="teoria/energia-corpo-rigido/definizioni">

## Definitions

The kinetic energy of a rigid body splits (König) into translation of the CM plus rotation about the CM: $K = \frac{1}{2}Mv_{cm}^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{cm}\omega^2$. For rotation about a fixed axis: $K = \frac{1}{2}I_{axis}\omega^2$. The mechanical energy is E = K + U with $U = Mgh_{cm}$ (the gravitational potential energy of a rigid body depends only on the height of the CM). If only conservative forces do work and the constraints do not dissipate, E is conserved.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/energia-corpo-rigido/derivazione" class="derivation">

## Step-by-step derivation

<p class="muted">Try deriving it aloud first, then open one step at a time.</p>

<details id="passo-1">
<summary>Step 1 · Kinetic energy for rotation about a fixed axis</summary>

$$
K = \sum_i \frac{1}{2}m_i v_i^2 = \sum_i \frac{1}{2}m_i(\omega r_i)^2 = \frac{1}{2}I_{axis}\,\omega^2
$$

Every point has v = ωrᵢ with the same ω: the moment of inertia collects all the geometry.

</details>

<details id="passo-2">
<summary>Step 2 · General case via König</summary>

$$
K = \frac{1}{2}Mv_{cm}^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{cm}\omega^2
$$

Translation + rotation about the CM. The two forms are consistent: for rotation about a fixed axis at distance d from the CM, ½I_axis ω² = ½(I_cm + Md²)ω² = ½Mv_cm² + ½I_cm ω², with v_cm = ωd.

</details>

<details id="passo-3">
<summary>Step 3 · Gravitational potential energy of the rigid body</summary>

$$
U = \sum_i m_i g z_i = g\sum_i m_i z_i = Mg\,z_{cm}
$$

In a uniform field the potential energy of the whole body is that of the total mass placed at the CM.

</details>

<details id="passo-4">
<summary>Step 4 · Kinetic energy theorem for the rigid body</summary>

$$
\Delta K = W^{ext}
$$

The work of internal forces is zero because of the rigidity constraint (distances do not change): only external work counts. For rotation: W = ∫τ dθ.

</details>

<details id="passo-5">
<summary>Step 5 · Conservation of mechanical energy</summary>

$$
\frac{1}{2}Mv_{cm}^2 + \frac{1}{2}I_{cm}\omega^2 + Mg\,z_{cm} = \text{const}
$$

Valid if the active forces are conservative and the constraint reactions do no work (smooth pivots, pure rolling).

</details>

<details id="passo-6">
<summary>Step 6 · Example: hinged rod falling from horizontal</summary>

$$
Mg\frac{L}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{3}ML^2\Big)\omega^2 \;\Rightarrow\; \omega = \sqrt{\frac{3g}{L}}
$$

The CM drops by L/2; the energy goes entirely into rotation about the pivot (I = ML²/3). A classic oral-exam example.

</details>

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/energia-corpo-rigido/ipotesi">

## Validity assumptions

Rigid body; uniform gravitational field; ideal (non-dissipative) constraints; for K = ½Iω² a fixed axis.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/energia-corpo-rigido/casi-limite">

## Limiting cases

Pure translation: K = ½Mv². Pure rotation about the CM: K = ½I_cmω². Pure rolling: both terms with the constraint v = ωR. Rough pivot or slipping: E is not conserved, ΔE = W_friction.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio" title="Worked example">

<div data-note-id="teoria/energia-corpo-rigido/esempio">

A uniform rod of length L = 1.2 m, hinged without friction at one end, is released from rest in the horizontal position. When it passes through the vertical, find: (a) the angular velocity; (b) the speed of the free end; (c) the speed of the centre of mass.

1. **Energy conservation.** $Mg\,\tfrac L2 = \tfrac12 \cdot \tfrac13 ML^2\omega^2 \Rightarrow \omega = \sqrt{3g/L} \approx 4.95\ \text{rad/s}$.
2. **Speeds.** End: $v = \omega L \approx 5.94\ \text{m/s}$; CM: $v_{cm} = \omega L/2 \approx 2.97\ \text{m/s}$.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori" title="Common mistakes">

<div data-note-id="teoria/energia-corpo-rigido/errori">

- Adding ½I_axis ω² (with I about the pivot) AND ½Mv_cm²: translation is counted twice.
- Using I_cm when the rotation is about an off-centre pivot (or vice versa) without Huygens–Steiner.
- Computing U with the height of an arbitrary point instead of the CM.
- Forgetting to check that the constraints do no work before conserving E.

</div>
