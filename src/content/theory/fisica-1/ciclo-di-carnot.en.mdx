<Layer kind="intuizione" title="The idea in brief">

<div data-note-id="teoria/ciclo-di-carnot/idea">

Carnot designs the 'perfect' engine by eliminating every waste: never a thermal contact across a finite temperature jump (that is why heat is exchanged only along isotherms) and never friction. Even so, part of the heat MUST be discharged to the cold reservoir: it is the minimum price, dictated by the ratio of temperatures, that no engineering can eliminate.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/ciclo-di-carnot/definizioni">

## Definitions

The Carnot cycle is the reversible cycle performed between only TWO thermal reservoirs (hot T*C, from Italian caldo, and cold T_F, from freddo), made of four quasi-static transformations: isothermal expansion at T_C, adiabatic expansion from T_C to T_F, isothermal compression at T_F, adiabatic compression back to T_C. For an ideal gas its efficiency is $\eta*{Carnot} = 1 - T_F/T_C$: it depends only on the temperatures of the reservoirs.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/ciclo-di-carnot/derivazione" class="derivation">

## Step-by-step derivation

<p class="muted">Try deriving it aloud first, then open one step at a time.</p>

<details id="passo-1">
<summary>Step 1 · Branch 1→2: isothermal expansion at T_C</summary>

$$
\Delta U = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; Q_C = W_{12} = nRT_C\ln\frac{V_2}{V_1} > 0
$$

The gas absorbs Q_C from the hot reservoir and converts it entirely into work (for this branch). Perfect thermal contact and infinite slowness are required: reversible.

</details>

<details id="passo-2">
<summary>Step 2 · Branch 2→3: adiabatic expansion</summary>

$$
Q = 0, \qquad W_{23} = -\Delta U = nc_V(T_C - T_F) > 0
$$

Thermally isolated, the gas keeps expanding while cooling from T_C to T_F: the work is paid for by internal energy.

</details>

<details id="passo-3">
<summary>Step 3 · Branch 3→4: isothermal compression at T_F</summary>

$$
Q_F = W_{34} = nRT_F\ln\frac{V_4}{V_3} < 0
$$

The gas is compressed, releasing to the cold reservoir the heat |Q_F| = nRT_F ln(V₃/V₄).

</details>

<details id="passo-4">
<summary>Step 4 · Branch 4→1: adiabatic compression</summary>

$$
Q = 0, \qquad W_{41} = -nc_V(T_C - T_F) < 0
$$

Adiabatic compression heats the gas back to T_C: it closes the cycle. The works of the two adiabatic branches cancel exactly.

</details>

<details id="passo-5">
<summary>Step 5 · Geometric constraint from the adiabatics</summary>

$$
T_CV_2^{\gamma-1} = T_FV_3^{\gamma-1}, \quad T_CV_1^{\gamma-1} = T_FV_4^{\gamma-1} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{V_2}{V_1} = \frac{V_3}{V_4}
$$

Dividing the two adiabatic relations: the isothermal expansion ratios are equal. This is the key step that lets the logarithms cancel.

</details>

<details id="passo-6">
<summary>Step 6 · Efficiency of the cycle</summary>

$$
\eta = 1 - \frac{|Q_F|}{Q_C} = 1 - \frac{nRT_F\ln(V_3/V_4)}{nRT_C\ln(V_2/V_1)} = 1 - \frac{T_F}{T_C}
$$

Thanks to the equality of the volume ratios the logarithms cancel: the efficiency depends ONLY on the temperatures. Immediate corollary: Q_C/T_C = |Q_F|/T_F (the 'reduced heats' balance).

</details>

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/ciclo-di-carnot/ipotesi">

## Validity assumptions

All transformations reversible (quasi-static, frictionless, heat exchanges across infinitesimal temperature differences); only two ideal reservoirs; for the explicit calculation, an ideal gas as working fluid (but the result is universal by Carnot's theorem).

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/ciclo-di-carnot/casi-limite">

## Limiting cases

T_F → T_C: η → 0, no temperature difference, no work. T_F → 0: η → 1, but absolute zero is unreachable (third law). Reversed cycle: Carnot refrigerator with COP = T_F/(T_C − T_F), the maximum possible.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio" title="Worked example">

<div data-note-id="teoria/ciclo-di-carnot/esempio">

0.20 mol of monatomic ideal gas performs a Carnot cycle between 600 K and 300 K. In the isothermal expansion the volume doubles. Find: (a) the absorbed heat Q_C; (b) the efficiency; (c) the net work per cycle; (d) the ratio between the maximum and minimum volume of the cycle.

1. **Hot isotherm.** $Q_C = nRT_C\ln 2 = 0.20 \cdot 8.314 \cdot 600 \cdot 0.693 \approx 691.5\ \text{J}$.
2. **Efficiency and work.** $\eta = 1 - 300/600 = 0.50$, $W = \eta Q_C \approx 345.8\ \text{J}$.
3. **Volumes.** Adiabatic 2→3: $V_3/V_2 = (T_C/T_F)^{1/(\gamma-1)} = 2^{3/2} \approx 2.83$. Hence $V_3/V_1 = 2 \cdot 2.83 \approx 5.66$.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori" title="Common mistakes">

<div data-note-id="teoria/ciclo-di-carnot/errori">

- Using temperatures in degrees Celsius in the efficiency.
- Saying that the efficiency depends on the gas or on the size of the cycle: only on the temperatures.
- Forgetting the proof of the constraint V₂/V₁ = V₃/V₄ (the examiner asks for it).
- Confusing the work of the single branches with the net work of the cycle (area enclosed in the p-V plane).

</div>
