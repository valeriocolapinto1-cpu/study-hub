<Layer kind="intuizione" title="The idea in brief">

<div data-note-id="teoria/teoria-cinetica-gas/idea">

The pressure of a gas is a dense drumming of molecules on the wall: each collision is a tiny push, and the average of billions of pushes per second is a steady force. Temperature, seen from here, stops being primitive: it is simply the macroscopic name for the average kinetic agitation of the molecules.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/teoria-cinetica-gas/definizioni">

## Definitions

Kinetic theory interprets the macroscopic quantities of a gas in terms of the disordered motion of its molecules. Ideal-gas model: N identical point-like molecules, not interacting except through instantaneous elastic collisions, in chaotic and isotropic motion. Central result: the pressure on the walls is $p = \frac{1}{3}\frac{N}{V}m\langle v^2\rangle$, i.e. two thirds of the translational kinetic energy density.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/teoria-cinetica-gas/derivazione" class="derivation">

## Step-by-step derivation

<p class="muted">Try deriving it aloud first, then open one step at a time.</p>

<details id="passo-1">
<summary>Step 1 · Elastic collision of a molecule with the wall</summary>

$$
\Delta p_x = -2mv_x
$$

A molecule with velocity component v_x towards the wall (perpendicular to x) bounces back elastically: it reverses v_x and transfers an impulse 2mv_x to the wall.

</details>

<details id="passo-2">
<summary>Step 2 · How many molecules hit in time dt</summary>

$$
dN_{urti} = \frac{1}{2}\,n_v\,A\,v_x\,dt
$$

The wall of area A is hit by the molecules within distance v_x dt that are travelling TOWARDS the wall (half of them, by isotropy); n_v = N/V is the number density. (Rigorous treatment: average over all v_x &gt; 0.)

</details>

<details id="passo-3">
<summary>Step 3 · Average force on the wall</summary>

$$
F = \Big\langle 2mv_x \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,n_v\,A\,v_x \Big\rangle \;\Rightarrow\; F = n_v\,m\,\langle v_x^2\rangle\,A
$$

Combining the impulse per collision and the collision rate, and averaging over v_x², the factors 2 and ½ cancel.

</details>

<details id="passo-4">
<summary>Step 4 · Pressure and isotropy</summary>

$$
p = \frac{F}{A} = n_v m\langle v_x^2\rangle; \qquad \langle v_x^2\rangle = \frac{1}{3}\langle v^2\rangle \;\Rightarrow\; p = \frac{1}{3}\,\frac{N}{V}\,m\langle v^2\rangle
$$

By isotropy ⟨v²⟩ = ⟨v_x²⟩+⟨v_y²⟩+⟨v_z²⟩ = 3⟨v_x²⟩: no direction is privileged.

</details>

<details id="passo-5">
<summary>Step 5 · Rewriting with the average kinetic energy</summary>

$$
pV = \frac{2}{3}N\,\Big\langle \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \Big\rangle
$$

Pressure times volume is 2/3 of the total translational kinetic energy: a bridge between micro and macro.

</details>

<details id="passo-6">
<summary>Step 6 · Comparison with the ideal-gas law: meaning of temperature</summary>

$$
pV = Nk_BT \;\Rightarrow\; \Big\langle \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \Big\rangle = \frac{3}{2}k_BT, \qquad v_{qm} = \sqrt{\frac{3k_BT}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M_{mol}}}
$$

Absolute temperature is the measure of the average translational kinetic energy of the molecules: the conceptually central result of the whole chapter.

</details>

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/teoria-cinetica-gas/ipotesi">

## Validity assumptions

Ideal gas: point-like molecules (negligible own volume), no action at a distance, elastic collisions; an enormous number of molecules; chaotic and isotropic motion; stationary velocity distribution; smooth walls.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/teoria-cinetica-gas/casi-limite">

## Limiting cases

High pressure or low temperature: the molecules' own volume and interactions are no longer negligible → real gases (Van der Waals). T → 0: v_rms → 0 (classically). Gas mixture: each species contributes its own partial pressure (Dalton's law, immediate from the derivation).

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio" title="Worked example">

<div data-note-id="teoria/teoria-cinetica-gas/esempio">

Find the root-mean-square speed of the molecules: (a) of helium (M = 4.0 g/mol) at 300 K; (b) of oxygen (M = 32 g/mol) at the same temperature; (c) the average translational kinetic energy per molecule at 300 K.

1. **RMS speeds.** $v_{rms} = \sqrt{3RT/M}$: helium $\sqrt{3\cdot 8.314\cdot 300/0.004} \approx 1368\ \text{m/s}$; oxygen $\approx 484\ \text{m/s}$ (ratio $\sqrt{8}$).
2. **Average energy.** $\tfrac32 k_BT = 1.5 \cdot 1.381\cdot 10^{-23} \cdot 300 \approx 6.21\cdot 10^{-21}\ \text{J}$, the same for all gases: at equal T lighter molecules are faster.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori" title="Common mistakes">

<div data-note-id="teoria/teoria-cinetica-gas/errori">

- Forgetting the factor 1/3 (isotropy) or the factor 2 of the impulse.
- Confusing average speed, root-mean-square speed and most probable speed.
- Saying that temperature measures the TOTAL energy of the molecules: it measures only the average translational kinetic energy.
- Thinking that all molecules have the same speed: there is a distribution (Maxwell–Boltzmann).

</div>
