<Layer kind="intuizione" title="The idea in brief">

<div data-note-id="teoria/statica-corpo-rigido/idea">

Equilibrium means that the forces do not push the body anywhere AND do not make it turn about any pivot. Freedom in choosing the pole is a tactical weapon: placing it where the unknown forces pass makes their torques vanish and simplifies the equation. Stability is geometry of the centre of gravity: a body topples when the vertical through its centre of gravity leaves the base of support.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/statica-corpo-rigido/definizioni">

## Definitions

A rigid body is in static equilibrium when it is at rest and stays at rest: all accelerations (linear and angular) are zero. There are two equilibrium conditions: zero resultant of the external forces $\big(\sum\vec{F} = \vec{0}\big)$ and zero resultant torque about any pole $\big(\sum\vec{\tau} = \vec{0}\big)$. In the plane they become 3 scalar equations: two for forces and one for torques.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/statica-corpo-rigido/derivazione" class="derivation">

## Step-by-step derivation

<p class="muted">Try deriving it aloud first, then open one step at a time.</p>

<details id="passo-1">
<summary>Step 1 · From the cardinal equations with zero accelerations</summary>

$$
\vec{a}_{cm} = \vec{0} \Rightarrow \sum\vec{F}^{ext} = \vec{0}; \qquad \frac{d\vec{L}}{dt} = \vec{0} \Rightarrow \sum\vec{\tau}^{ext} = \vec{0}
$$

Equilibrium is the special case of dynamics with constant P and L (both zero for a body initially at rest).

</details>

<details id="passo-2">
<summary>Step 2 · Independence of the pole (if the resultant is zero)</summary>

$$
\vec{\tau}_{O'} = \vec{\tau}_{O} + (\vec{O'O})\times\sum\vec{F} = \vec{\tau}_{O}
$$

When ΣF = 0, the total torque is the same about any pole: you can choose the most convenient one (typically where most unknown forces pass, to eliminate them).

</details>

<details id="passo-3">
<summary>Step 3 · Weight acts at the centre of gravity</summary>

$$
\vec{\tau}_{weight} = \sum_i \vec{r}_i\times m_i\vec{g} = \Big(\sum_i m_i\vec{r}_i\Big)\times\vec{g} = \vec{r}_{cm}\times M\vec{g}
$$

In a uniform field the total torque of the weight is that of a single force Mg applied at the centre of mass: this is why centre of gravity and CM coincide.

</details>

<details id="passo-4">
<summary>Step 4 · Guiding example: ladder leaning against a wall</summary>

$$
N_w = f_s,\quad N_f = Mg,\quad (\text{pole on the ground}):\; N_w L\sin\theta = Mg\frac{L}{2}\cos\theta \;\Rightarrow\; f_s = \frac{Mg}{2\tan\theta} \le \mu_s Mg
$$

Smooth wall, rough floor: the three equations (x, y, torques) determine the reactions; the no-slip condition gives the minimum angle tan θ ≥ 1/(2μ_s).

</details>

<details id="passo-5">
<summary>Step 5 · Stability of equilibrium</summary>

$$
\frac{dU}{dq} = 0; \qquad \frac{d^2U}{dq^2} > 0 \text{ stable}, \quad < 0 \text{ unstable}
$$

An equilibrium is stable if small displacements raise the centre of gravity (U grows): the body comes back. Practical criterion: the vertical through the centre of gravity must fall inside the base of support.

</details>

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/statica-corpo-rigido/ipotesi">

## Validity assumptions

Perfectly rigid body; uniform gravitational field (for centre of gravity ≡ CM); smooth or rough constraints modelled with normal reactions and static friction ≤ μ_sN.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/statica-corpo-rigido/casi-limite">

## Limiting cases

A body on the verge of tipping: the normal reaction concentrates on the edge. Insufficient friction: equilibrium is impossible and the body slides. A couple: zero resultant but non-zero torque — no equilibrium, the body rotates.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio" title="Worked example">

<div data-note-id="teoria/statica-corpo-rigido/esempio">

A uniform ladder of mass 12 kg and length 4.0 m leans against a smooth wall at an angle of 60° with the rough floor. Find: (a) the reaction of the wall; (b) the static friction force needed; (c) the minimum friction coefficient for it not to slip.

1. **Torques about the foot.** $N_w L\sin 60° = Mg\,\tfrac{L}{2}\cos 60° \Rightarrow N_w = \dfrac{Mg}{2\tan 60°} \approx 33.98\ \text{N}$.
2. **Forces.** Horizontal: $f_s = N_w \approx 33.98\ \text{N}$; vertical: $N_f = Mg \approx 117.7\ \text{N}$.
3. **Minimum friction.** $f_s \le \mu_s N_f \Rightarrow \mu_s \ge \dfrac{1}{2\tan 60°} \approx 0.289$, independent of mass and length.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori" title="Common mistakes">

<div data-note-id="teoria/statica-corpo-rigido/errori">

- Imposing only ΣF = 0 and forgetting torques (a couple has zero resultant but makes things rotate).
- Changing pole halfway through the torque calculation.
- Getting the lever arm wrong (using the distance from the point of application instead of from the line of action).
- Forgetting that static friction is an unknown ≤ μ_sN, not equal to μ_sN except at the verge of slipping.

</div>
