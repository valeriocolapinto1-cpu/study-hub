<Layer kind="intuizione" title="The idea in brief">

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-koenig/idea">

König separates the motion 'of the system as a whole' from the 'internal' motion. It is why we can say that the Earth has an orbital angular momentum around the Sun plus a spin angular momentum about its own axis, or that the energy of a rolling ball is translation plus rotation: the mathematics guarantees that the two contributions simply add up, with no cross terms.

</div>

<Layer kind="formalismo" title="Definitions and derivation">

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-koenig/definizioni">

## Definitions

König's theorems split the angular momentum and kinetic energy of a system into the sum of a 'centre-of-mass' term (as if all the mass were concentrated at the CM) and a term 'relative to the centre of mass' (internal motion seen from the CM frame): $\vec{L} = \vec{r}_{cm}\times M\vec{v}_{cm} + \vec{L}'$ and $K = \frac{1}{2}Mv_{cm}^2 + K'$. The CM frame is the frame with origin at the CM and axes parallel to the inertial ones.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-koenig/derivazione" class="derivation">

## Step-by-step derivation

<p class="muted">Try deriving it aloud first, then open one step at a time.</p>

<details id="passo-1">
<summary>Step 1 · Decomposition of the coordinates relative to the CM</summary>

$$
\vec{r}_i = \vec{r}_{cm} + \vec{r}_i{'}, \qquad \vec{v}_i = \vec{v}_{cm} + \vec{v}_i{'}
$$

Primed quantities are measured in the centre-of-mass frame. Key property: $\sum_i m_i\vec{r}_i{'} = \vec{0}$ and, differentiating, $\sum_i m_i\vec{v}_i{'} = \vec{0}$.

</details>

<details id="passo-2">
<summary>Step 2 · König for angular momentum: expansion</summary>

$$
\vec{L} = \sum_i (\vec{r}_{cm} + \vec{r}_i{'})\times m_i(\vec{v}_{cm} + \vec{v}_i{'})
$$

Four terms: two 'diagonal' and two mixed.

</details>

<details id="passo-3">
<summary>Step 3 · The mixed terms vanish</summary>

$$
\vec{r}_{cm}\times\Big(\sum_i m_i\vec{v}_i{'}\Big) = \vec{0}, \qquad \Big(\sum_i m_i\vec{r}_i{'}\Big)\times\vec{v}_{cm} = \vec{0}
$$

Both sums are zero by definition of centre of mass.

</details>

<details id="passo-4">
<summary>Step 4 · König's first theorem</summary>

$$
\vec{L} = \vec{r}_{cm}\times M\vec{v}_{cm} + \sum_i \vec{r}_i{'}\times m_i\vec{v}_i{'} = \vec{L}_{cm} + \vec{L}'
$$

'Orbital' angular momentum of the CM + 'spin' angular momentum about the CM. L' is intrinsic: it does not depend on the pole.

</details>

<details id="passo-5">
<summary>Step 5 · König for kinetic energy: expansion</summary>

$$
K = \sum_i \frac{1}{2}m_i(\vec{v}_{cm} + \vec{v}_i{'})\cdot(\vec{v}_{cm} + \vec{v}_i{'}) = \frac{1}{2}Mv_{cm}^2 + \vec{v}_{cm}\cdot\sum_i m_i\vec{v}_i{'} + \sum_i\frac{1}{2}m_iv_i'^2
$$

Here too the mixed term contains Σmᵢv'ᵢ = 0.

</details>

<details id="passo-6">
<summary>Step 6 · König's second theorem</summary>

$$
K = \frac{1}{2}Mv_{cm}^2 + K'
$$

Translational energy of the CM + energy of the motion about the CM. For a rigid body K' = ½I_cm ω²: hence K = ½Mv_cm² + ½I_cm ω² for rolling.

</details>

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-koenig/ipotesi">

## Validity assumptions

CM reference frame in pure translation (axes parallel to the inertial ones, even if the CM accelerates); constant masses. No rigidity assumption for the general theorems.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-koenig/casi-limite">

## Limiting cases

A body that only translates: K' = 0, K = ½Mv_cm². A body rotating about a CM at rest: K = ½I_cmω². Two-body system: K' = ½μv_rel² with μ the reduced mass — the decomposition used in collisions.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio" title="Worked example">

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-koenig/esempio">

A bicycle wheel (mass 1.5 kg concentrated practically on the rim of radius 0.35 m) rolls without slipping at 18 km/h. Find: (a) the translational kinetic energy; (b) the rotational kinetic energy about the CM; (c) the rotational fraction of the total energy.

1. **Translation.** 18 km/h = 5.0 m/s: $K_{cm} = \tfrac12 Mv^2 = 18.75\ \text{J}$.
2. **Rotation.** Ring: $I_{cm} = MR^2$, rolling $\omega = v/R$: $K' = \tfrac12 MR^2 (v/R)^2 = 18.75\ \text{J}$.
3. **Fraction.** For a ring the split is exactly half and half: rotational fraction 0.50, independent of v and R.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori" title="Common mistakes">

<div data-note-id="teoria/teorema-di-koenig/errori">

- Forgetting one of the two terms (typically: counting only translation in rolling).
- Counting the rotational energy twice by using I about the contact point AND adding translation.
- Believing that L' depends on the pole: it is intrinsic to the system.
- Applying König with a relative frame that rotates: it must only translate with the CM.

</div>
