<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="leggi-di-kirchhoff-intuizione-1">

Charge does not pile up in wires and nodes: whatever current flows in must flow out. And if you walk around a closed path back to where you started, you find the same potential again: the voltage "rises" and "drops" cancel out. These two ideas, charge conservation and a well-defined potential, are all you need.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="leggi-di-kirchhoff-teoria-1">

**Kirchhoff's current law (KCL).** At every instant the algebraic sum of the currents of the branches meeting at a node is zero: $\sum_k \pm i_k = 0$, with sign $+$ for leaving currents and $-$ for entering ones (or vice versa, as long as you are consistent).

</div>

<div data-note-id="leggi-di-kirchhoff-teoria-2">

**Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL).** At every instant the algebraic sum of the branch voltages around a loop, traversed in one direction, is zero: $\sum_k \pm v_k = 0$, with $+$ when the reference direction of the voltage agrees with the direction of traversal.

</div>

<div data-note-id="leggi-di-kirchhoff-teoria-3">

Both laws depend only on the _topology_ (how the branches are connected), not on the nature of the components. They hold at every instant, for any waveform, within the lumped-parameter model.

</div>

<div data-note-id="leggi-di-kirchhoff-teoria-4">

**Extension to cut sets.** A _cut set_ is a set of branches whose removal splits the circuit into two parts: KCL holds for every cut set (algebraic sum of the currents crossing the closed surface that encloses one of the two parts).

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="leggi-di-kirchhoff-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: KCL holds for every cut set</summary>

**Statement.** Let $S$ be a closed surface enclosing a set of nodes: the algebraic sum of the currents of the branches crossing it is zero.

1. Write KCL for each node enclosed by $S$: $\sum_{k\in\text{node}} \pm i_k = 0$.
2. Add up the equations of all the inner nodes.
3. A branch lying entirely inside $S$ joins two inner nodes: its current appears in two equations with opposite signs (it leaves one node and enters the other) and cancels out.
4. Only the currents of the branches crossing $S$ remain, with consistent signs (what leaves the inner node leaves $S$). Their sum is zero. $\square$

</details>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="leggi-di-kirchhoff-esempio-1">

### Numerical KCL and KVL

Currents $i_1 = 3\,\mathrm{A}$ and $i_2 = 2\,\mathrm{A}$ enter a node, while $i_3 = 4\,\mathrm{A}$ and $i_4$ leave it. Find $i_4$. Then, in a three-branch loop, $v_1 = 5\,\mathrm{V}$ and $v_2 = -2\,\mathrm{V}$ (directions agreeing with the traversal): find $v_3$.

**Solution.**

1. KCL (entering $=$ leaving): $3+2 = 4 + i_4 \Rightarrow i_4 = 1\,\mathrm{A}$.
2. KVL: $v_1+v_2+v_3 = 0 \Rightarrow v_3 = -(5-2) = -3\,\mathrm{V}$.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="leggi-di-kirchhoff-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Writing KCL with mixed signs without fixing a convention, obtaining $i_1+i_2+i_3=0$ with all currents entering but positive.
- **Right:** Fix "leaving $+$, entering $-$" and derive the signs from the reference directions, not from the values.

</div>
