<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="fasori-e-laplace-intuizione-1">

Phasors and Laplace are not two separate worlds: phasors are Laplace "restricted" to the imaginary axis, $s=j\omega$, without initial conditions. That is why they only see the steady state, and only if a steady state exists.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="fasori-e-laplace-teoria-1">

The network function for phasors is $H(j\omega)=H(s)\big|_{s=j\omega}$, valid for stable systems. The magnitude $|H(j\omega)|$ and the phase $\arg H(j\omega)$ tell how the steady-state sinusoidal output is scaled and shifted with respect to the input.

</div>

<div data-note-id="fasori-e-laplace-teoria-2">

The phasor equations are the Laplace ones with $s=j\omega$ and _without_ initial conditions: the phasor method only sees the steady state.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="fasori-e-laplace-esempio-1">

### Magnitude and phase

$H=\dfrac{1}{s+1}$ at $\omega=1$.

**Solution.**

1. $H(j)=\dfrac1{1+j}$: $|H|=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\approx0.707$, $\arg=-45^\circ$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="fasori-e-laplace-esempio-2">

### High-pass

$H=\dfrac{s}{s+1}$ at $\omega=1$.

**Solution.**

1. $\dfrac{j}{1+j}=\dfrac{j(1-j)}2=\dfrac{1+j}{2}$: $|H|=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}$, $\arg=+45^\circ$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="fasori-e-laplace-esempio-3">

### Steady-state output

$x=4\cos t$ in the first system.

**Solution.**

1. $y_p=4\cdot0.707\cos(t-45^\circ)\approx2.83\cos(t-45^\circ)$.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="fasori-e-laplace-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Using $H(j\omega)$ for unstable systems.
- **Right:** The relation holds only with poles with negative real part.

</div>

</Layer>
