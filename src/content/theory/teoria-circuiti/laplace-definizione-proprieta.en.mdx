<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="laplace-definizione-proprieta-intuizione-1">

The Laplace transform "photographs" a signal by decomposing it into complex exponentials $e^{st}$. In this new domain differentiating becomes multiplying by $s$ and the initial conditions show up by themselves: a differential equation is solved with algebra.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="laplace-definizione-proprieta-teoria-1">

The unilateral transform of $x(t)$ (zero for $t<0^-$) is $X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty}x(t)e^{-st}\,dt$, with $s=\sigma+j\omega$, defined in the half-plane of convergence. The lower limit $0^-$ includes impulses at $t=0$ and makes the initial conditions $x(0^-)$ natural.

</div>

<div data-note-id="laplace-definizione-proprieta-teoria-2">

**Properties.** Linearity: $ax+by\to aX+bY$. Derivative: $\dot x\to sX-x(0^-)$; $\ddot x\to s^2X-sx(0^-)-\dot x(0^-)$. Integral: $\int_{0^-}^{t}x\to X/s$. Time delay: $x(t-T)u_{-1}(t-T)\to e^{-sT}X$. Frequency shift: $e^{-at}x\to X(s+a)$. Convolution: $x*y\to XY$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="laplace-definizione-proprieta-teoria-3">

**Value theorems.** Initial value: $x(0^+)=\lim_{s\to\infty}sX(s)$. Final value: $\lim_{t\to\infty}x(t)=\lim_{s\to0}sX(s)$ if all the poles of $sX(s)$ have negative real part.

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="laplace-definizione-proprieta-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: Transform of the derivative</summary>

**Statement.** $\mathcal L\{\dot x\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)$.

1. By definition $\mathcal L\{\dot x\}=\int_{0^-}^{\infty}\dot x(t)e^{-st}dt$.
2. Integrating by parts: $\left[x(t)e^{-st}\right]_{0^-}^{\infty}+s\int_{0^-}^{\infty}x(t)e^{-st}dt$.
3. In the region of convergence the term at infinity vanishes: what remains is $-x(0^-)+sX(s)$. $\square$

</details>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="laplace-definizione-proprieta-esempio-1">

### Derivative of an exponential

$x=e^{-2t}u_{-1}(t)$, $x(0^-)=0$: check $\mathcal L\{\dot x\}$.

**Solution.**

1. $X=1/(s+2)$; $sX-0=\dfrac{s}{s+2}$.
2. Directly: $\dot x=-2e^{-2t}u_{-1}+\delta(t)\to\dfrac{-2}{s+2}+1=\dfrac{s}{s+2}$ ✓ (the impulse appears thanks to the $0^-$ limit).

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="laplace-definizione-proprieta-esempio-2">

### Initial and final values

$X(s)=\dfrac{3}{s(s+1)}$.

**Solution.**

1. $sX=\dfrac3{s+1}$: $x(0^+)=\lim_{s\to\infty}=0$, $x(\infty)=\lim_{s\to0}=3$ (the pole $-1$ is stable).

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="laplace-definizione-proprieta-esempio-3">

### Time delay

$x(t)=e^{-t}u_{-1}(t)$ delayed by $2$.

**Solution.**

1. $\mathcal L\{x(t-2)u_{-1}(t-2)\}=\dfrac{e^{-2s}}{s+1}$.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="laplace-definizione-proprieta-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Using the final-value theorem with poles on the imaginary axis.
- **Right:** $sX(s)$ must have all its poles in the open left half-plane.

</div>

<div data-note-id="laplace-definizione-proprieta-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Forgetting $x(0^-)$ in the transform of the derivative.
- **Right:** $\dot x\to sX-x(0^-)$.

</div>

</Layer>
