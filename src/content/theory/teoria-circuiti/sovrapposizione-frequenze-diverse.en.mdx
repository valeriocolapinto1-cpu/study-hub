<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-intuizione-1">

A phasor only makes sense together with its frequency: two phasors at different frequencies are like two quantities in different units, they do not add. Solve the circuit once per frequency, then add the responses in time, where everything is comparable again.

</div>

<div data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-intuizione-2">

It is like an orchestra: each instrument plays its own note and the circuit responds to each one differently, because impedances and phase shifts depend on frequency. What you hear in the end is the sum of the responses, but to compute them you must listen to one note at a time.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-teoria-1">

In a linear circuit with several sources at different frequencies (DC is frequency zero) the response is the sum of the responses to each source, each computed with its own method: DC with $C$ open and $L$ shorted, sinusoidal with phasors at that $\omega$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-teoria-2">

Never add phasors at different frequencies: they are numbers attached to different $e^{j\omega_kt}$. The sum is taken on the time-domain expressions.

</div>

<div data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-teoria-3">

**Initial condition in the mixed case.** $x(0^-)=x_{dc}+\sum_k\mathrm{Re}\{X_k\}$ (the value at $t=0$ of each term $\mathrm{Re}\{X_ke^{j\omega_kt}\}$ is $\mathrm{Re}\{X_k\}$).

</div>

<div data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-teoria-4">

**Procedure.** (1) Switch off all sources except those at a given angular frequency $\omega_k$ (voltage → short, current → open). (2) Solve the circuit with phasors at $\omega_k$; in DC with $C$ open and $L$ shorted. (3) Go back to time: $x_k(t)=\mathrm{Re}\{X_ke^{j\omega_kt}\}$. (4) Add: $x(t)=x_{dc}+\sum_kx_k(t)$. Different sources at the _same_ frequency are instead handled together, in a single phasor analysis.

</div>

<div data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-teoria-5">

**Average power.** For average power different frequencies do not interfere: products of sinusoids at different frequencies have zero mean, so $P=P_{dc}+\sum_kP_k$. For the same reason the overall RMS value is $X_{rms}=\sqrt{X_{dc}^2+\sum_kX_{k,rms}^2}$.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-esempio-1">

### DC plus sinusoid

Series RC ($R=C=1$), $v_g=4+\cos t$ for $t<0$. Find $v_C(t)$ for $t<0$ and $v_C(0^-)$.

**Solution.**

1. DC: $v_C^{dc}=4$.
2. Sinusoidal ($\omega=1$): $\tfrac12\cos t+\tfrac12\sin t$.
3. $v_C(t)=4+\tfrac12\cos t+\tfrac12\sin t$; $v_C(0^-)=4+\tfrac12=\tfrac92$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-esempio-2">

### Two sinusoids

Initial condition for $v_C=\tfrac12\cos t+\tfrac12\sin t+\tfrac15\cos2t+\tfrac25\sin2t$.

**Solution.**

1. Value at $t=0$: $\tfrac12+\tfrac15=\tfrac7{10}$ (the sines are $0$).

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-esempio-3">

### A typical mistake

A student adds the magnitudes, $|X_1|+|X_2|$, as the total amplitude. Why is this wrong?

**Solution.**

1. The two sinusoids have different frequencies, so the total amplitude is not the sum of the magnitudes: the time-domain expression is the sum of the two sinusoids. The value at $t=0$ is the sum of the instantaneous values.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-esempio-4">

### DC and sinusoid on an RL circuit

$R=1\,\Omega$ in series with $L=1\,\mathrm H$, $v_g=2+2\cos t\,\mathrm V$. Find the steady-state $i(t)$ and the average power in $R$.

**Solution.**

1. DC ($L$ shorted): $i_{dc}=2/1=2\,\mathrm A$.
2. $\omega=1$: $\dot I=\dfrac{2}{1+j}=1-j$ (peak), so $i_1=\cos t+\sin t=\sqrt2\cos(t-45^\circ)$.
3. $i(t)=2+\cos t+\sin t$, with $i(0)=3\,\mathrm A$. Average power: $P=R\left(i_{dc}^2+\tfrac12|\dot I|^2\right)=1\cdot(4+1)=5\,\mathrm W$.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Using the magnitude of the phasor as the initial condition.
- **Right:** You need the value at $t=0$: the real part of the phasor.

</div>

<div data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Computing the average power as $R\,(i_{dc}+|\dot I|)^2$.
- **Right:** With different frequencies the cross terms have zero mean: $P=R\left(i_{dc}^2+\tfrac12|\dot I|^2\right)$ with peak phasors.

</div>

<div data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** In the DC analysis keeping the capacitor as $1/(j\omega C)$ with the $\omega$ of the sinusoidal source.
- **Right:** Each analysis uses its own angular frequency: for $\omega=0$ the capacitor is an open circuit and the inductor a short.

</div>

<div data-note-id="sovrapposizione-frequenze-diverse-errore-4">

- **Wrong:** Computing the RMS value of the sum as the sum of the RMS values.
- **Right:** With different frequencies $X_{rms}=\sqrt{X_{dc}^2+\sum_kX_{k,rms}^2}$: in the example $I_{rms}=\sqrt{4+1}=\sqrt5\approx2.24\,\mathrm A$, not $2+1=3\,\mathrm A$.

</div>
