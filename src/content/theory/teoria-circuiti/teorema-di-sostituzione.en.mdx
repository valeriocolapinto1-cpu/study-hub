<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-sostituzione-intuizione-1">

If you already know the voltage and current on a piece of circuit, the rest of the circuit "does not notice" if you replace it with a source imposing exactly that value. A very simple idea, but it is the first brick of Thévenin's and Norton's theorems.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-sostituzione-teoria-1">

**Substitution theorem.** If a circuit has a unique solution and one of its one-ports (or ports) has voltage $v^*$ and current $i^*$ in that solution, it can be replaced by an ideal voltage source $v^*$ or by an ideal current source $i^*$ without changing the voltages and currents of the rest of the circuit, provided that the modified circuit still has a unique solution.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-sostituzione-teoria-2">

The theorem does not require linearity. It is the basis for Thévenin, Norton and for analysis by parts (a part is replaced by an equivalent source).

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="teorema-di-sostituzione-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: Substitution with a source</summary>

1. Let $(\mathbf v^*,\mathbf i^*)$ be the solution of the original circuit.
2. In the modified circuit the equations (KCL, KVL and the constitutive relations of the remaining elements) are the same as before, except for the relation of the replaced one-port, which becomes $v=v^*$ (or $i=i^*$).
3. $(\mathbf v^*,\mathbf i^*)$ satisfies all of them: the replaced one-port has exactly $v=v^*$. If the solution of the modified circuit is unique, it coincides with $(\mathbf v^*,\mathbf i^*)$. $\square$

</details>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-sostituzione-esempio-1">

### Replacing a resistor

$V=12$, $R_1=2$, $R_2=4$ in series; the voltage across $R_2$ is $8\,\mathrm V$. Replace $R_2$.

**Solution.**

1. $R_2\to$ an $8\,\mathrm V$ voltage source: the current in $R_1$ is $\dfrac{12-8}{2}=2\,\mathrm A$, as before ($12/6$).

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-sostituzione-esempio-2">

### Substitution with a current source

Same circuit: $R_2\to$ a $2\,\mathrm A$ current source.

**Solution.**

1. The voltage across $R_1$ is $2\cdot2=4\,\mathrm V$, that across the source $8\,\mathrm V$: identical ✓.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-sostituzione-esempio-3">

### When it does not hold

Replacing $R_2$ with a voltage source $v^*$ in parallel with another, different voltage source.

**Solution.**

1. It would violate KVL: the modified circuit has no solution, so the theorem does not apply.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-sostituzione-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Applying substitution without checking that the solution is unique.
- **Right:** The modified circuit must have a unique solution.

</div>
