<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="fasori-intuizione-1">

Every quantity of a linear circuit driven by a sinusoid oscillates at the same frequency: only amplitude and phase change. The phasor stores exactly these two pieces of information in a complex number, and turns derivatives and integrals into simple multiplications: differential equations become algebraic.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="fasori-teoria-1">

To $v(t)=V_m\cos(\omega t+\varphi)$ we associate the **phasor** $\dot V=V_me^{j\varphi}$, with $v(t)=\mathrm{Re}\{\dot Ve^{j\omega t}\}$. The phasor is a constant complex number: the time dependence lies in the factor $e^{j\omega t}$, common to all quantities and left implicit.

</div>

<div data-note-id="fasori-teoria-2">

The correspondence is linear: a sum of sinusoids at the same frequency ↔ the sum of the phasors. Derivative ↔ multiplication by $j\omega$, integral ↔ division by $j\omega$. The sine is reduced to a cosine: $\sin\omega t=\cos(\omega t-90^\circ)$, phasor $-j$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="fasori-teoria-3">

Cartesian ↔ amplitude-phase form: $a\cos\omega t+b\sin\omega t=\mathrm{Re}\{(a-jb)e^{j\omega t}\}=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\cos(\omega t-\arctan(b/a))$ (for $a>0$; in general $\varphi=\mathrm{atan2}(-b,a)$).

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="fasori-esempio-1">

### Sum of a sine and a cosine

Write $3\cos t+4\sin t$ in amplitude-phase form.

**Solution.**

1. Phasor: $3-4j$, magnitude $5$, phase $-\arctan(4/3)\approx-53.13^\circ$.
2. $3\cos t+4\sin t=5\cos(t-53.13^\circ)$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="fasori-esempio-2">

### Derivative of a sinusoid

$v(t)=10\cos(2t+30^\circ)$. Find $\dot v$.

**Solution.**

1. $\dot V=10e^{j30^\circ}$; $j\omega\dot V=2e^{j90^\circ}\cdot10e^{j30^\circ}=20e^{j120^\circ}$.
2. $\dot v=20\cos(2t+120^\circ)=-20\sin(2t+30^\circ)$ ✓.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="fasori-esempio-3">

### Phasor of a sine

What is the phasor of $5\sin(3t)$ in the cosine convention?

**Solution.**

1. $5\sin3t=5\cos(3t-90^\circ)$, hence $\dot V=5e^{-j90^\circ}=-5j$.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="fasori-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Forgetting that the sine has phase $-90^\circ$ with respect to the cosine and using the phasor $+5j$.
- **Right:** $\sin\omega t\leftrightarrow-j$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="fasori-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Confusing peak and RMS amplitude: $V_m=\sqrt2V$.
- **Right:** Always state whether phasors are peak or RMS values (it changes $P$, $Q$).

</div>

</Layer>
