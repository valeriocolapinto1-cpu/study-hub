<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-intuizione-1">

The $Z$ and $Y$ matrices treat the two ports alike; the transmission matrix instead describes the two-port as a "stretch of road" carrying quantities from the output back to the input. It is the natural choice when two-ports are lined up one after another.

</div>

<div data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-intuizione-2">

Each representation answers a different question. $Z$: which voltages do given currents produce? $Y$: which currents do given voltages produce? Transmission: what is needed at the input to get a given output? Hybrid: the most convenient variables for a transistor, current at the input and voltage at the output.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-1">

**Inverse transmission matrix** (ABCD): $\begin{pmatrix}v_1\\i_1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v_2\\-i_2\end{pmatrix}$. From $Z$: $A=\dfrac{z_{11}}{z_{21}}$, $B=\dfrac{\Delta_z}{z_{21}}$, $C=\dfrac{1}{z_{21}}$, $D=\dfrac{z_{22}}{z_{21}}$ with $\Delta_z=z_{11}z_{22}-z_{12}z_{21}$. Reciprocal network: $AD-BC=1$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-2">

**Hybrid** parameters $h$: $v_1=h_{11}i_1+h_{12}v_2$, $i_2=h_{21}i_1+h_{22}v_2$ (typical of transistors).

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: From $Z$ to the transmission matrix</summary>

1. From $v_2=z_{21}i_1+z_{22}i_2$: $i_1=\dfrac{v_2-z_{22}i_2}{z_{21}}=\dfrac1{z_{21}}v_2+\dfrac{z_{22}}{z_{21}}(-i_2)$: $C=1/z_{21}$, $D=z_{22}/z_{21}$.
2. Substituting into $v_1=z_{11}i_1+z_{12}i_2$: $v_1=\dfrac{z_{11}}{z_{21}}v_2+\left(\dfrac{z_{11}z_{22}}{z_{21}}-z_{12}\right)(-i_2)$.
3. Hence $A=z_{11}/z_{21}$ and $B=\Delta_z/z_{21}$. $\square$

</details>

<div data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-3">

**Hybrid parameters.** $h_{11}=v_1/i_1|_{v_2=0}$ is an input impedance with the output shorted, $h_{21}=i_2/i_1|_{v_2=0}$ a current gain, $h_{12}=v_1/v_2|_{i_1=0}$ a reverse voltage gain, $h_{22}=i_2/v_2|_{i_1=0}$ an output admittance with the input open: quantities of different nature, hence the name "hybrid". For a reciprocal network $h_{12}=-h_{21}$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-4">

**Transmission properties.** For a reciprocal network $AD-BC=1$: the determinant is a quick check of the algebra. $A$ and $D$ are dimensionless, $B$ is an impedance, $C$ an admittance; with port 2 open $A=v_1/v_2$ and $C=i_1/v_2$, with port 2 shorted $B=v_1/(-i_2)$ and $D=i_1/(-i_2)$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-5">

**When a representation is missing.** A matrix exists only if the variables chosen as independent can be set freely. A resistor in series between the ports forces $i_1=-i_2$ and has no $Z$, but has $Y$ and $T$; a shunt resistor forces $v_1=v_2$ and has no $Y$, but has $Z$ and $T$; the ideal transformer has $T$ and $h$ but neither $Z$ nor $Y$.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-esempio-1">

### T network

$Z=\begin{pmatrix}4&3\\3&5\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.**

1. $\Delta_z=20-9=11$: $A=\tfrac43$, $B=\tfrac{11}3$, $C=\tfrac13$, $D=\tfrac53$.
2. $AD-BC=\tfrac{20}{9}-\tfrac{11}{9}=1$ ✓.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-esempio-2">

### Series resistor

$R=2$ in series on the upper branch.

**Solution.**

1. $v_1=v_2+R(-i_2)$, $i_1=-i_2$: $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-esempio-3">

### Shunt conductance

$G=\tfrac13$ between the two branches.

**Solution.**

1. $v_1=v_2$, $i_1=Gv_2+(-i_2)$: $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1/3&1\end{pmatrix}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-esempio-4">

### Hybrid parameters of the T network

For the T network with $1$, $2$ and $3\,\Omega$, $Z=\begin{pmatrix}4&3\\3&5\end{pmatrix}$. Find the $h$ parameters and check two of them on the circuit.

**Solution.**

1. From the formulas: $h_{11}=\Delta_z/z_{22}=11/5=2.2\,\Omega$, $h_{12}=z_{12}/z_{22}=0.6$, $h_{21}=-z_{21}/z_{22}=-0.6$, $h_{22}=1/z_{22}=0.2\,\mathrm S$.
2. Check of $h_{11}$: with port 2 shorted, port 1 sees $1+3\parallel2=1+1.2=2.2\,\Omega$ ✓.
3. Check of $h_{21}$: current divider, the $2\,\Omega$ branch towards the short carries $i_1\cdot\tfrac35=0.6\,i_1$, which leaves port 2: $i_2=-0.6\,i_1$ ✓; and $h_{12}=-h_{21}$ (reciprocal network) ✓.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Using $i_2$ instead of $-i_2$ in the transmission matrix.
- **Right:** The current $-i_2$ leaves port 2 (towards the load).

</div>

<div data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Taking for granted that every representation exists.
- **Right:** A resistor in series between the ports has no $Z$, a shunt one has no $Y$: before computing, check that the chosen independent variables can be set freely.

</div>

<div data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Confusing the transmission matrix with its inverse.
- **Right:** The one used in the course gives the input quantities $(v_1,i_1)$ in terms of the output ones $(v_2,-i_2)$; the other does the opposite and has different entries.

</div>

<div data-note-id="altre-rappresentazioni-doppi-bipoli-errore-4">

- **Wrong:** Computing $\det T$ of a network with controlled sources and expecting $1$.
- **Right:** $AD-BC=1$ holds for reciprocal networks; with controlled sources the determinant is in general different from $1$.

</div>
