<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-intuizione-1">

Impedance is the "Ohm's law" of sinusoidal steady state: a single complex number tells how much a one-port scales the amplitude and how much it shifts the phase. With impedances everything you can do with resistors (series, parallel, dividers, Thévenin) still works, just with complex numbers.

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-intuizione-2">

The sign of the reactance tells who chases whom. In the inductor the current arrives late, because the voltage must first make it grow; in the capacitor the current arrives early, because it is the current that charges it. A one-port with $X\gt 0$ behaves "like an inductor", with $X\lt 0$ "like a capacitor", and with $X=0$ it is purely resistive (or at resonance).

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-teoria-1">

In sinusoidal steady state a linear one-port has **impedance** $Z=\dot V/\dot I$ (passive sign convention) and **admittance** $Y=1/Z$. $Z=R+jX$: $R$ resistance, $X$ reactance; $Y=G+jB$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-teoria-2">

$Z_R=R$, $Z_L=j\omega L$, $Z_C=\dfrac1{j\omega C}=-\dfrac{j}{\omega C}$. The **fictitious circuit in the phasor domain** is obtained by replacing every one-port with its impedance and every source with its phasor: KCL, KVL, series, parallel, dividers, Thévenin, nodes and meshes all hold with complex numbers.

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-teoria-3">

An inductor has positive reactance (the current lags the voltage), a capacitor negative. At low frequency $C$ behaves as an open circuit and $L$ as a short; at high frequency the opposite.

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-teoria-4">

**Polar form.** $Z=|Z|e^{j\varphi}$ with $|Z|=\sqrt{R^2+X^2}$ and $\varphi=\arctan(X/R)$ (for $R\gt 0$): the modulus is the ratio of the voltage and current amplitudes, $\varphi$ is the lead of the voltage over the current. For a passive one-port $R\ge0$, so $-90^\circ\le\varphi\le90^\circ$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-teoria-5">

**From $Z$ to $Y$.** $Y=\dfrac1{R+jX}=\dfrac{R-jX}{R^2+X^2}$, so $G=\dfrac{R}{R^2+X^2}$ and $B=-\dfrac{X}{R^2+X^2}$. The conductance is not $1/R$ (it is only when $X=0$) and the susceptance has the opposite sign of the reactance.

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-teoria-6">

**Frequency dependence.** $|Z_L|=\omega L$ grows with $\omega$, $|Z_C|=1/(\omega C)$ decreases. In a series RL or RC there is an angular frequency where reactance and resistance have the same magnitude ($\omega=R/L$ or $\omega=1/(RC)$): there the phase is $\pm45^\circ$, which is the origin of the cutoff frequency of first-order filters.

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-teoria-7">

**Equivalent at one frequency.** A passive one-port with $Z=R+jX$ at a given angular frequency can be replaced by a resistor $R$ in series with an inductor $L=X/\omega$ (if $X\gt 0$) or a capacitor $C=-1/(\omega X)$ (if $X\lt 0$). The equivalence holds only at that frequency.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-esempio-1">

### Series RL

$R=3\,\Omega$, $L=2\,\mathrm H$, $\omega=2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Find $Z$ and $Y$.

**Solution.**

1. $Z=3+j\cdot2\cdot2=3+4j=5e^{j53.13^\circ}$.
2. $Y=\dfrac1Z=\dfrac{3-4j}{25}=0.12-0.16j\,\mathrm S$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-esempio-2">

### Parallel RC

$R=1\,\Omega$ in parallel with $C=1\,\mathrm F$, $\omega=1$. Find $Z$.

**Solution.**

1. $Y=1+j$, $Z=\dfrac1{1+j}=\tfrac12-\tfrac12j$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-esempio-3">

### Complex divider

$R=1$ in series with $C=1$ ($\omega=1$), $\dot V_g=1$. What is the capacitor voltage?

**Solution.**

1. $\dot V_C=\dot V_g\dfrac{-j}{1-j}=\dfrac{-j(1+j)}{2}=\dfrac{1-j}{2}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-esempio-4">

### Series RLC at a given frequency

$R=10\,\Omega$, $L=0.1\,\mathrm H$, $C=100\,\mu\mathrm F$ in series, $\omega=200\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, RMS voltage $V=100\,\mathrm V$. Find $Z$, the current and the phase shift.

**Solution.**

1. $X_L=\omega L=20\,\Omega$, $X_C=-\dfrac{1}{\omega C}=-\dfrac{1}{200\cdot10^{-4}}=-50\,\Omega$.
2. $Z=10+j(20-50)=10-30j$, $|Z|=\sqrt{100+900}=31.62\,\Omega$, $\varphi=\arctan(-3)=-71.57^\circ$.
3. $I=V/|Z|=100/31.62=3.16\,\mathrm A$, leading the voltage by $71.57^\circ$: at this frequency the circuit is capacitive ($X\lt 0$).

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Writing $Z_C=j\omega C$.
- **Right:** $Z_C=1/(j\omega C)=-j/(\omega C)$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Computing the conductance as $G=1/R$ even when there is reactance.
- **Right:** $G=\mathrm{Re}\{1/Z\}=R/(R^2+X^2)$: it equals $1/R$ only if $X=0$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Adding the magnitudes of series impedances: $|Z|=R+|X_L|+|X_C|$.
- **Right:** Add the complex numbers: the reactances of $L$ and $C$ have opposite signs and partly cancel (in the example $20-50=-30$).

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-errore-4">

- **Wrong:** Using $Z_L=j\omega L$ with the frequency in hertz in place of $\omega$.
- **Right:** $\omega=2\pi f$ in rad/s: at $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$ a $0.1\,\mathrm H$ inductor has $|Z_L|=31.4\,\Omega$, not $5\,\Omega$.

</div>

</Layer>
