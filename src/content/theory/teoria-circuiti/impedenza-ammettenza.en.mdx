<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-intuizione-1">

Impedance is the "Ohm's law" of sinusoidal steady state: a single complex number tells how much a one-port scales the amplitude and how much it shifts the phase. With impedances everything you can do with resistors (series, parallel, dividers, Thévenin) still works, just with complex numbers.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-teoria-1">

In sinusoidal steady state a linear one-port has **impedance** $Z=\dot V/\dot I$ (passive sign convention) and **admittance** $Y=1/Z$. $Z=R+jX$: $R$ resistance, $X$ reactance; $Y=G+jB$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-teoria-2">

$Z_R=R$, $Z_L=j\omega L$, $Z_C=\dfrac1{j\omega C}=-\dfrac{j}{\omega C}$. The **fictitious circuit in the phasor domain** is obtained by replacing every one-port with its impedance and every source with its phasor: KCL, KVL, series, parallel, dividers, Thévenin, nodes and meshes all hold with complex numbers.

</div>

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-teoria-3">

An inductor has positive reactance (the current lags the voltage), a capacitor negative. At low frequency $C$ behaves as an open circuit and $L$ as a short; at high frequency the opposite.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-esempio-1">

### Series RL

$R=3\,\Omega$, $L=2\,\mathrm H$, $\omega=2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Find $Z$ and $Y$.

**Solution.**

1. $Z=3+j\cdot2\cdot2=3+4j=5e^{j53.13^\circ}$.
2. $Y=\dfrac1Z=\dfrac{3-4j}{25}=0.12-0.16j\,\mathrm S$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-esempio-2">

### Parallel RC

$R=1\,\Omega$ in parallel with $C=1\,\mathrm F$, $\omega=1$. Find $Z$.

**Solution.**

1. $Y=1+j$, $Z=\dfrac1{1+j}=\tfrac12-\tfrac12j$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-esempio-3">

### Complex divider

$R=1$ in series with $C=1$ ($\omega=1$), $\dot V_g=1$. What is the capacitor voltage?

**Solution.**

1. $\dot V_C=\dot V_g\dfrac{-j}{1-j}=\dfrac{-j(1+j)}{2}=\dfrac{1-j}{2}$.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="impedenza-ammettenza-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Writing $Z_C=j\omega C$.
- **Right:** $Z_C=1/(j\omega C)=-j/(\omega C)$.

</div>
