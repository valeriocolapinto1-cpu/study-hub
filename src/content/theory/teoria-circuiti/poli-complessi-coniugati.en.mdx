<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="poli-complessi-coniugati-intuizione-1">

Two complex-conjugate poles are the signature of an oscillation: the imaginary part gives the angular frequency, the real part tells whether the amplitude dies out, stays constant or grows. Writing the denominator as $(s+\alpha)^2+\omega^2$ makes both visible.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="poli-complessi-coniugati-teoria-1">

Poles $-\alpha\pm j\omega$ come in conjugate pairs for real coefficients. Write the denominator as $(s+\alpha)^2+\omega^2$ and the numerator as $a(s+\alpha)+c$:

$$
\mathcal L^{-1}\left\{\frac{as+b}{(s+\alpha)^2+\omega^2}\right\}=e^{-\alpha t}\left[a\cos\omega t+\frac{b-a\alpha}{\omega}\sin\omega t\right].
$$

</div>

<div data-note-id="poli-complessi-coniugati-teoria-2">

$\alpha>0$: damped oscillation; $\alpha=0$: sustained (marginal) oscillation; $\alpha<0$: growing (unstable).

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="poli-complessi-coniugati-esempio-1">

### Purely imaginary poles

$F(s)=\dfrac{2s+6}{s^2+9}$.

**Solution.**

1. $\alpha=0$, $\omega=3$, $a=2$, $b=6$: $f=2\cos3t+2\sin3t$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="poli-complessi-coniugati-esempio-2">

### Poles with a real part

$F(s)=\dfrac{s+3}{s^2+2s+5}$.

**Solution.**

1. $(s+1)^2+4$: $\alpha=1$, $\omega=2$, $a=1$, $b=3$.
2. $f=e^{-t}\left[\cos2t+\dfrac{3-1}{2}\sin2t\right]=e^{-t}(\cos2t+\sin2t)$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="poli-complessi-coniugati-esempio-3">

### Amplitude and phase

Write $e^{-t}(\cos2t+\sin2t)$ in amplitude-phase form.

**Solution.**

1. $\cos2t+\sin2t=\sqrt2\cos(2t-45^\circ)$.
2. $f=\sqrt2e^{-t}\cos(2t-45^\circ)$.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="poli-complessi-coniugati-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Factoring $s^2+2s+5$ into real factors.
- **Right:** It has complex roots: complete the square.

</div>
