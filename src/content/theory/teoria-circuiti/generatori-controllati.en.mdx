<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-intuizione-1">

A controlled source is a "tap" operated by another quantity of the circuit: its output is not fixed from outside but copies, amplified, a voltage or current found elsewhere. That is why it is not a true signal source and stays in place even when the independent sources are switched off.

</div>

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-intuizione-2">

A controlled source models what a transistor or an amplifier does: a small signal at the input commands a large quantity at the output, and the energy needed comes from the power supply, which the model does not draw. This is why a controlled source can deliver net power: it is the sign of a hidden source behind it.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-teoria-1">

A **controlled source** is a two-port: the quantity at one port (input, controlling port) determines the quantity at the other (output). The four linear types:

</div>

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-teoria-2">

- **VCVS** (voltage ↔ voltage): $v_2=\mu\,v_1$, $i_1=0$.
- **VCCS** (voltage → current): $i_2=g_m\,v_1$, $i_1=0$.
- **CCVS** (current → voltage): $v_2=r_m\,i_1$, $v_1=0$.
- **CCCS** (current ↔ current): $i_2=\beta\,i_1$, $v_1=0$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-teoria-3">

They are _active_ elements (they can deliver net power) and _non-reciprocal_. They are used to model amplifiers, transistors, etc.

</div>

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-teoria-4">

**Working rule.** In circuits with controlled sources, when the "independent sources are switched off" (for Thévenin, Norton, zero-input response) the controlled sources stay in the circuit.

</div>

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-teoria-5">

**In the equations.** In nodal (or mesh) analysis a controlled source is first written as an independent source of unknown value; then an equation is added expressing the controlling quantity in terms of the unknowns of the method. The system matrix in general loses its symmetry: this is the trace of non-reciprocity.

</div>

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-teoria-6">

**Units of the parameters.** $\mu$ (VCVS) and $\beta$ (CCCS) are dimensionless; $g_m$ (VCCS) is a conductance, in siemens; $r_m$ (CCVS) is a resistance, in ohms. Checking units is a quick way to recognise which of the four types is in use.

</div>

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-teoria-7">

**A controlled source that becomes a resistor.** If the controlling quantity is the voltage (or current) of the source's own port, the source is equivalent to a resistor: a VCCS absorbing $g\,v$, with $v$ its own voltage, is a resistor of value $1/g$, negative if $g\lt 0$. This is the case of the example on the [Thévenin's theorem](../teorema-di-thevenin/) page.

</div>

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-teoria-8">

**Negative resistance.** A resistor $R$ in parallel with a CCCS that injects into the top node the current $2\,i_R$, where $i_R$ is the resistor current: the current absorbed by the port is $i=i_R-2\,i_R=-v/R$, so the port is equivalent to $R_{eq}=-R$. With passive elements alone this could never happen.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="generatori-controllati-esempio-1">

### VCCS with an upstream divider

A source $V_g=6\,\mathrm{V}$ feeds $R_1=2\,\Omega$ in series with $R_2=4\,\Omega$; the voltage $v_2$ across $R_2$ controls a VCCS with $g_m=0.5\,\mathrm{S}$ feeding $R_3=3\,\Omega$. Find the voltage across $R_3$.

**Solution.**

1. The control is a voltage and draws no current: $v_2 = 6\cdot\dfrac{4}{2+4}=4\,\mathrm{V}$.
2. $i_{out}=g_m v_2=0.5\cdot4=2\,\mathrm{A}$.
3. $v_3=R_3\,i_{out}=3\cdot2=6\,\mathrm{V}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="generatori-controllati-esempio-2">

### Power gain of a VCVS

A VCVS with $\mu=20$ has an input voltage $v_1=0.5\,\mathrm V$ (taken without drawing current) and a load $R_L=100\,\Omega$ at the output. Find $v_2$, the load current and power, and the power supplied at the input.

**Solution.**

1. $v_2=\mu\,v_1=20\cdot0.5=10\,\mathrm V$.
2. $i_L=v_2/R_L=10/100=0.1\,\mathrm A$; $P_L=v_2\,i_L=1\,\mathrm W$.
3. At the input $i_1=0$, so $P_1=v_1 i_1=0$: the watt in the load comes entirely from the controlled source, which delivers net power (an active element).

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Switching off a VCCS as well when finding $R_{th}$ ("it is a source").
- **Right:** Only _independent_ sources are switched off; the controlled one stays and $R_{th}$ is found with the test source $V/I$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Mixing up VCCS and CCVS because both "mix" voltage and current.
- **Right:** The name reads control → output: a VCCS is a current commanded by a voltage ($i_2=g_m v_1$, $g_m$ in siemens); a CCVS is a voltage commanded by a current ($v_2=r_m i_1$, $r_m$ in ohms).

</div>

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Forgetting the equation of the controlling quantity in nodal analysis.
- **Right:** Each controlled source adds a term that depends on a circuit quantity: the equation linking it to the node voltages is needed, otherwise the system has more unknowns than equations.

</div>

<div data-note-id="generatori-controllati-errore-4">

- **Wrong:** Taking for granted that a circuit of positive resistors and controlled sources has $R_{eq}\gt 0$.
- **Right:** Controlled sources can deliver net power: the equivalent resistance can turn out negative, as in the CCCS case above.

</div>

</Layer>
