<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-intuizione-1">

Connecting two-ports is like combining resistors, but with matrices: in series the impedances add, in parallel the admittances, in cascade the transmissions "compose". You only have to pick the representation suited to the type of connection.

</div>

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-intuizione-2">

The rule is remembered by looking at what the two networks share after the connection. In series-series they share the port currents and the voltages add: the $Z$ matrices add. In parallel-parallel they share the voltages and the currents add: the $Y$ matrices add. In cascade the output of one is the input of the other: the $T$ matrices multiply.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-1">

**Series-series** (ports in series): $Z=Z_a+Z_b$. **Parallel-parallel** (ports in parallel): $Y=Y_a+Y_b$. **Cascade** (port 2 of $a$ on port 1 of $b$): $T=T_aT_b$. The first two hold only if the validity test (VI-08) is passed.

</div>

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-2">

In a cascade the reference directions matter: the output current of the first network is $-i_2^{(a)}$ and it is the input current $i_1^{(b)}$ of the second; this is why the transmission matrix uses $(v_2,-i_2)$ and the matrices are multiplied in the order in which the networks are connected.

</div>

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-3">

**Why the cascade multiplies.** For the first network $\begin{pmatrix}v_1\\i_1\end{pmatrix}=T_a\begin{pmatrix}v_2^{(a)}\\-i_2^{(a)}\end{pmatrix}$; the connection imposes $v_2^{(a)}=v_1^{(b)}$ and $-i_2^{(a)}=i_1^{(b)}$, i.e. the output vector of $a$ is the input vector of $b$, which equals $T_b\begin{pmatrix}v_2\\-i_2\end{pmatrix}$. Substituting gives $T=T_aT_b$. Order matters: in general $T_aT_b\neq T_bT_a$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-4">

**Mixed connections.** Series-parallel (inputs in series, outputs in parallel) and parallel-series are handled the same way with hybrid matrices: one adds the $h$ matrices and their inverses $g$ respectively, always after checking that the connection is valid.

</div>

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-5">

**Matrices of the elementary blocks.** An impedance $Z$ in series between the ports has $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&Z\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, a shunt admittance $Y$ has $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\Y&1\end{pmatrix}$, an ideal transformer $T=\begin{pmatrix}n&0\\0&1/n\end{pmatrix}$. Any ladder network is obtained by multiplying these matrices in the order in which the blocks appear from the source to the load.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-esempio-1">

### Cascade

Series $R=2$ followed by shunt $G=\tfrac13$.

**Solution.**

1. $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1/3&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}5/3&2\\1/3&1\end{pmatrix}$.
2. With port 2 open ($i_2=0$): $Z_{in}=A/C=5\,\Omega$ (check: $2+3$ ✓).

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-esempio-2">

### Parallel-parallel

$Y_a=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}$, $Y_b=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.**

1. $Y=\begin{pmatrix}3&-1\\-1&3\end{pmatrix}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-esempio-3">

### Series-series

$Z_a=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$, $Z_b=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.**

1. $Z=\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\2&5\end{pmatrix}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-esempio-4">

### Cascade in reverse order

Same blocks as the previous example, but first the shunt $G=\tfrac13$ and then the series $R=2$. Find $T$ and the input impedance with port 2 open.

**Solution.**

1. $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1/3&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\1/3&5/3\end{pmatrix}$; $\det T=\tfrac53-\tfrac23=1$ ✓.
2. Port 2 open: $Z_{in}=A/C=1/(1/3)=3\,\Omega$.
3. Check on the circuit: the input sees the $3\,\Omega$ shunt directly, while the $2\,\Omega$ series element is open at the far end. In the previous order it was $5\,\Omega$: the order of the cascade matters.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Multiplying the $Z$ matrices in a cascade.
- **Right:** Cascade: multiply the transmission matrices.

</div>

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Forgetting that in a cascade the order of the matrices matters.
- **Right:** $T=T_aT_b$ with $a$ the network on the source side: swapping the order changes the circuit (in the example $Z_{in}$ goes from $5$ to $3\,\Omega$).

</div>

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Adding the $Y$ matrices of two networks connected in series-series.
- **Right:** In series-series the $Z$ matrices add, because the port currents are shared and the voltages add; the $Y$ matrices add in parallel-parallel.

</div>

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-errore-4">

- **Wrong:** Using the sum formulas without checking that the connection is valid.
- **Right:** Series-series and parallel-parallel require every port to remain a port after the connection: this is checked with the [validity tests](../prove-validita-connessioni/).

</div>

</Layer>
