<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-intuizione-1">

Connecting two-ports is like combining resistors, but with matrices: in series the impedances add, in parallel the admittances, in cascade the transmissions "compose". You only have to pick the representation suited to the type of connection.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-1">

**Series-series** (ports in series): $Z=Z_a+Z_b$. **Parallel-parallel** (ports in parallel): $Y=Y_a+Y_b$. **Cascade** (port 2 of $a$ on port 1 of $b$): $T=T_aT_b$. The first two hold only if the validity test (VI-08) is passed.

</div>

<div data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-teoria-2">

In a cascade the reference directions matter: the output current of the first network is $-i_2^{(a)}$ and it is the input current $i_1^{(b)}$ of the second; this is why the transmission matrix uses $(v_2,-i_2)$ and the matrices are multiplied in the order in which the networks are connected.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-esempio-1">

### Cascade

Series $R=2$ followed by shunt $G=\tfrac13$.

**Solution.**

1. $T=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1/3&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}5/3&2\\1/3&1\end{pmatrix}$.
2. With port 2 open ($i_2=0$): $Z_{in}=A/C=5\,\Omega$ (check: $2+3$ ✓).

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-esempio-2">

### Parallel-parallel

$Y_a=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}$, $Y_b=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.**

1. $Y=\begin{pmatrix}3&-1\\-1&3\end{pmatrix}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="connessioni-doppi-bipoli-esempio-3">

### Series-series

$Z_a=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$, $Z_b=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution.**

1. $Z=\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\2&5\end{pmatrix}$.

</div>
