<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-intuizione-1">

The response of a circuit has two "causes": the energy it already stored (initial conditions) and what reaches it from outside (inputs). By linearity the two effects can be computed separately and then added.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-intuizione-2">

This distinction does not coincide with the one between transient and steady state. The free response is entirely transient (in a stable system), but the forced response contains both the steady state and a piece of transient: the piece needed for the output to start from zero when the initial conditions are zero.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-intuizione-3">

In practice the split is handy when the same circuit must be studied with several inputs: the free response is computed once, the forced one is recomputed for each input and added.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-teoria-1">

**Zero-input** (free) response: zero input, non-zero initial conditions (it depends only on the natural modes, i.e. on the poles). **Zero-state** (forced) response: zero initial conditions and non-zero input. In a linear system the complete response is the sum $y=y_{zi}+y_{zs}$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-teoria-2">

With Laplace: in the transformed circuit set the initial conditions to zero (zero-state) or the inputs to zero (zero-input).

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-teoria-3">

**Two linearities.** The free response is linear in the initial conditions (doubling $v_C(0^-)$ doubles it), the forced response is linear in the inputs. The complete response, if the initial conditions are nonzero, is not linear in the input alone: this is why linearity is stated "with zero initial conditions".

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-teoria-4">

**In the $s$ domain.** The transformed circuit has two kinds of sources: those of the inputs and those of the initial conditions. Switching off the latter gives the forced response $Y_f(s)=H(s)X(s)$, switching off the former the free response $Y_l(s)$. Both share the same denominator (the natural modes); the forced one also contains the poles of the input.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-teoria-5">

**Two ways to cut the same sum.** Complete response $=$ free $+$ forced $=$ transient $+$ steady state. The first split is by cause (initial state or input), the second by behaviour in time (what dies out and what remains); see [transient and steady-state response](../risposta-transitoria-permanente/).

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-esempio-1">

### RC with a step

$R=2$, $C=\tfrac12$, $v_C(0)=2$, input $10u_{-1}$.

**Solution.**

1. Zero-input: $v_C=2e^{-t}$.
2. Zero-state: $10(1-e^{-t})$.
3. Sum: $10-8e^{-t}$ ✓.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-esempio-2">

### Zero-input only

RL with $R=4$, $L=2$, $i(0)=3$, sources off.

**Solution.**

1. $i=3e^{-2t}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-esempio-3">

### Zero-state only

$H=\dfrac1{s+1}$ with $x=u_{-1}$, zero initial conditions.

**Solution.**

1. $Y=\dfrac1{s(s+1)}\Rightarrow y=1-e^{-t}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-esempio-4">

### RL: the two splits compared

$R=2\,\Omega$ in series with $L=1\,\mathrm H$, $i(0^-)=3\,\mathrm A$, $v_g=10\,u_{-1}(t)$. Find free, forced, transient and steady-state responses.

**Solution.**

1. $\tau=L/R=0.5\,\mathrm s$, $i_\infty=10/2=5\,\mathrm A$. Free: $3e^{-2t}$; forced: $5(1-e^{-2t})$.
2. Complete: $i=3e^{-2t}+5-5e^{-2t}=5-2e^{-2t}$; with Laplace $I=\dfrac{10/s+3}{s+2}=\dfrac5s-\dfrac2{s+2}$ ✓.
3. Steady state $5\,\mathrm A$, transient $-2e^{-2t}$: the transient mixes the free response ($+3e^{-2t}$) and the transient part of the forced one ($-5e^{-2t}$). Check: $i(0)=5-2=3$ ✓.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Attributing the transient to the zero-input response alone.
- **Right:** The zero-state response also contains transient terms ($-10e^{-t}$ above).

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Computing the forced response with the problem's initial conditions.
- **Right:** The forced response is computed with zero initial conditions; the initial state enters only the free response.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Thinking that the free response depends on the input.
- **Right:** The free response has the circuit's natural modes and amplitudes fixed by the initial conditions alone: the input plays no part.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-libera-forzata-errore-4">

- **Wrong:** Adding free and forced responses when the forced one was computed with the true initial conditions.
- **Right:** This counts the initial state twice: in the example one would get $i(0)=6\,\mathrm A$ instead of $3\,\mathrm A$. Always check the value at $t=0$.

</div>

</Layer>
