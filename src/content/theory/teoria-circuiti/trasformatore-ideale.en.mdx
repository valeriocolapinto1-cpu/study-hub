<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-intuizione-1">

The ideal transformer is an electrical "lever": it multiplies voltage by $n$ and divides current by $n$, so their product (the power) is unchanged. A load seen "through the lever" looks larger or smaller by a factor $n^2$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-intuizione-2">

The ideal transformer neither stores nor dissipates energy: everything that enters one port leaves the other, instant by instant. This is why it is used to "match" a load: by changing $n$ one chooses which resistance the source sees, without losing power in between.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-teoria-1">

The **ideal transformer** with ratio $n$ is a linear, memoryless two-port. With the passive sign convention on both ports:

$$
v_1 = n\,v_2,\qquad i_1 = -\frac{i_2}{n}.
$$

</div>

<div data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-teoria-2">

It is not an inductor: it also works in DC and it is a limiting model (perfect coupling, infinite inductances, no losses).

</div>

<div data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-teoria-3">

**Properties.** The total absorbed power is zero at every instant; a load $Z_L$ at port 2 is seen from port 1 as $n^2 Z_L$ (with $n$ defined as above).

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: The ideal transformer absorbs no power and reflects impedances</summary>

**Statement.** With $v_1=nv_2$ and $i_1=-i_2/n$: (a) $p_1+p_2=0$; (b) if $v_2=-Z_L\,i_2$ then $v_1/i_1=n^2Z_L$.

1. (a) $p=v_1 i_1+v_2 i_2 = (n v_2)(-i_2/n)+v_2 i_2 = -v_2 i_2+v_2 i_2 = 0$.
2. (b) Port 2 is closed on $Z_L$; with $i_2$ entering the transformer, the load current is $-i_2$, hence $v_2=Z_L(-i_2)$.
3. $Z_{in}=\dfrac{v_1}{i_1}=\dfrac{n\,v_2}{-i_2/n}=n^2\,\dfrac{v_2}{-i_2}=n^2 Z_L$. $\square$

</details>

<div data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-teoria-4">

**Zero power, a check.** With $v_1=n\,v_2$ and $i_1=-i_2/n$: $p=v_1i_1+v_2i_2=n\,v_2\cdot(-i_2/n)+v_2\,i_2=0$ at every instant. The ideal transformer is therefore lossless and memoryless: unlike two coupled inductors it stores no magnetic energy, and that is why it also transfers DC.

</div>

<div data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-teoria-5">

**Reflected load.** A load $Z_L$ at port 2 carries the current $-i_2$ (the one leaving the transformer), so $v_2=-Z_L\,i_2$ and $\dfrac{v_1}{i_1}=\dfrac{n\,v_2}{-i_2/n}=n^2\dfrac{v_2}{-i_2}=n^2Z_L$. Conversely, an impedance at port 1 seen from port 2 is $Z/n^2$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-teoria-6">

**As a two-port.** The ideal transformer has neither a $Z$ nor a $Y$ matrix: its relations give neither the voltages in terms of the currents nor the reverse. It does have a hybrid representation: $v_1=n\,v_2$ and $i_2=-n\,i_1$, i.e. $\begin{pmatrix}v_1\\i_2\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&n\\-n&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}i_1\\v_2\end{pmatrix}$.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-esempio-1">

### Transformer with a resistive load

$n=2$, load $R_L=3\,\Omega$ at port 2; at port 1 a $24\,\mathrm{V}$ source in series with $12\,\Omega$. Find $v_2$ and the load power.

**Solution.**

1. $R_{in}=n^2R_L=12\,\Omega$.
2. Primary loop: $24=12\,i_1+12\,i_1\Rightarrow i_1=1\,\mathrm{A}$, $v_1=12\,\mathrm{V}$.
3. $v_2=v_1/n=6\,\mathrm{V}$, $i_2=-n\,i_1=-2\,\mathrm{A}$ (the load current is $2\,\mathrm{A}$).
4. $P_L=6\cdot2=12\,\mathrm{W}=v_1 i_1$: power is conserved. ✓

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-esempio-2">

### Matching a loudspeaker

A source $E=10\,\mathrm V$ with internal resistance $R_s=800\,\Omega$ must feed a loudspeaker $R_L=8\,\Omega$. Which $n$ maximises the load power, and how much is it?

**Solution.**

1. We need $n^2R_L=R_s$: $n=\sqrt{800/8}=10$.
2. Primary side: $i_1=\dfrac{10}{800+800}=6.25\,\mathrm{mA}$, $v_1=800\cdot i_1=5\,\mathrm V$.
3. Load side: $v_2=v_1/n=0.5\,\mathrm V$, $P_L=v_2^2/R_L=0.25/8=31.25\,\mathrm{mW}$.
4. Check: $v_1 i_1=5\cdot6.25\,\mathrm{mW}=31.25\,\mathrm{mW}=\dfrac{E^2}{4R_s}$ ✓. Without the transformer: $i=10/808$ and $P_L=8\,i^2\approx1.2\,\mathrm{mW}$.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Using $i_1=i_2/n$ with both currents entering.
- **Right:** With the passive convention on both ports, $i_1=-i_2/n$ (the minus sign compensates for the directions).

</div>

<div data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Thinking that the ideal transformer works only with AC.
- **Right:** The ideal model also holds in DC; it is the real transformer, made of coupled inductors, that does not transfer DC.

</div>

<div data-note-id="trasformatore-ideale-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Reflecting a load as $Z_L/n^2$ instead of $n^2Z_L$, or vice versa.
- **Right:** With $v_1=n\,v_2$ the load at port 2 seen from port 1 is $n^2Z_L$; with the opposite definition of $n$ the factor is inverted. Check with the case $n=1$ and with the direction of the voltage ratio.

</div>

</Layer>
