<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-intuizione-1">

A filter is a circuit that treats frequencies differently: an RC with the output across the capacitor lets slow variations through and "flattens" fast ones, because the capacitor cannot keep up with them. The cut-off frequency marks the border between the two regions.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-intuizione-2">

The Bode plot makes filters easy to read: on a logarithmic scale the magnitude of a first-order filter is almost flat up to $\omega_c$ and then falls in a straight line by $20\,\mathrm{dB}$ per decade. Two asymptotes and a corner rounded by $3\,\mathrm{dB}$ sum up the whole behaviour.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-intuizione-3">

The high-pass filter mirrors the low-pass one: with the output across the resistor instead of the capacitor, fast variations pass and DC is blocked.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-teoria-1">

**First-order low-pass** $H=\dfrac{1}{1+s/\omega_c}$: $|H(j\omega)|=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega/\omega_c)^2}}$, phase $-\arctan(\omega/\omega_c)$. At $\omega_c$ the magnitude is $1/\sqrt2$ ($-3\,\mathrm{dB}$) and the phase $-45^\circ$; beyond $\omega_c$ it falls by $20\,\mathrm{dB/dec}$. RC: $\omega_c=1/(RC)$; RL (output across $R$): $\omega_c=R/L$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-teoria-2">

**High-pass** $H=\dfrac{s/\omega_c}{1+s/\omega_c}$: it blocks DC and passes high frequencies. The gain in dB is $20\log_{10}|H|$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-teoria-3">

**Decibels.** $20\log_{10}|H|$: a factor $10$ is $20\,\mathrm{dB}$, a factor $2$ about $6\,\mathrm{dB}$, $1/\sqrt2$ about $-3\,\mathrm{dB}$. Magnitudes that multiply become decibels that add: the plots of cascaded blocks (with no interaction between blocks) are obtained by adding those of the single blocks.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-teoria-4">

**Low-pass asymptotes.** For $\omega\ll\omega_c$ $|H|\approx1$ ($0\,\mathrm{dB}$) and phase about $0$; for $\omega\gg\omega_c$ $|H|\approx\omega_c/\omega$, i.e. $-20\,\mathrm{dB}$ per decade, and phase about $-90^\circ$. The asymptotes' largest error is at $\omega_c$, where the true magnitude is $-3\,\mathrm{dB}$ and the phase $-45^\circ$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-teoria-5">

**Higher orders and band-pass.** A second-order filter falls by $40\,\mathrm{dB}$ per decade; a band-pass (for instance an RLC with output across the resistor) passes frequencies near $\omega_0$ and attenuates both low and high ones, with bandwidth $B=\omega_0/Q$ (see [resonant circuits](../circuiti-risonanti/)).

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-teoria-6">

**Phase and delay.** A phase shift $\varphi$ at angular frequency $\omega$ corresponds to a delay $-\varphi/\omega$ of the output wave. In the low-pass filter at $\omega_c$ the phase is $-45^\circ=-\pi/4$, so the output is delayed by $\pi/(4\omega_c)$, one eighth of a period.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-esempio-1">

### Cut-off of an RC

$R=2\,\Omega$, $C=\tfrac12\,\mathrm F$.

**Solution.**

1. $\omega_c=\dfrac1{RC}=1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, $f_c=\dfrac{\omega_c}{2\pi}\approx0.159\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-esempio-2">

### Gain one decade above

Low-pass with $\omega_c=1$: $|H|$ at $\omega=10$.

**Solution.**

1. $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{101}}\approx0.0995\to-20.04\,\mathrm{dB}$ ($\approx-20\,\mathrm{dB}$).

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-esempio-3">

### Type of filter

$H=\dfrac{s}{s+2}$.

**Solution.**

1. $H(0)=0$, $|H(\infty)|=1$: high-pass with $\omega_c=2$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-esempio-4">

### Designing an RC low-pass filter

We want $f_c=1\,\mathrm{kHz}$ with $C=100\,\mathrm{nF}$. Find $R$ and the gain at $500\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and at $10\,\mathrm{kHz}$.

**Solution.**

1. $R=\dfrac{1}{2\pi f_cC}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi\cdot10^3\cdot10^{-7}}=1592\,\Omega$.
2. At $500\,\mathrm{Hz}$ ($f/f_c=0.5$): $|H|=1/\sqrt{1.25}=0.894$, i.e. $-0.97\,\mathrm{dB}$, phase $-26.6^\circ$.
3. At $10\,\mathrm{kHz}$ ($f/f_c=10$): $|H|=1/\sqrt{101}=0.0995$, i.e. $-20.0\,\mathrm{dB}$, phase $-84.3^\circ$: one decade above cutoff, $20\,\mathrm{dB}$ less ✓.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Saying that the 3 dB cut-off frequency is where the magnitude is half its maximum.
- **Right:** It is where the magnitude is $|H|_{max}/\sqrt2$ (half power).

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Confusing frequency and angular frequency: $f_c=1/(RC)$.
- **Right:** $\omega_c=1/(RC)$ in rad/s and $f_c=\dfrac{1}{2\pi RC}$ in hertz: they differ by a factor $2\pi\approx6.28$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Computing the decibels of a voltage ratio with $10\log_{10}$.
- **Right:** For voltages and currents use $20\log_{10}|H|$; $10\log_{10}$ is for power ratios.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-errore-4">

- **Wrong:** Connecting a load to the output of an RC filter and still using $\omega_c=1/(RC)$.
- **Right:** The load changes the resistance seen by the capacitor: the cutoff becomes $1/((R\parallel R_L)C)$ and the passband gain drops to $R_L/(R+R_L)$.

</div>
