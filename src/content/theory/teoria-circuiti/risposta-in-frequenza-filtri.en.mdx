<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-intuizione-1">

A filter is a circuit that treats frequencies differently: an RC with the output across the capacitor lets slow variations through and "flattens" fast ones, because the capacitor cannot keep up with them. The cut-off frequency marks the border between the two regions.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-teoria-1">

**First-order low-pass** $H=\dfrac{1}{1+s/\omega_c}$: $|H(j\omega)|=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega/\omega_c)^2}}$, phase $-\arctan(\omega/\omega_c)$. At $\omega_c$ the magnitude is $1/\sqrt2$ ($-3\,\mathrm{dB}$) and the phase $-45^\circ$; beyond $\omega_c$ it falls by $20\,\mathrm{dB/dec}$. RC: $\omega_c=1/(RC)$; RL (output across $R$): $\omega_c=R/L$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-teoria-2">

**High-pass** $H=\dfrac{s/\omega_c}{1+s/\omega_c}$: it blocks DC and passes high frequencies. The gain in dB is $20\log_{10}|H|$.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-esempio-1">

### Cut-off of an RC

$R=2\,\Omega$, $C=\tfrac12\,\mathrm F$.

**Solution.**

1. $\omega_c=\dfrac1{RC}=1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, $f_c=\dfrac{\omega_c}{2\pi}\approx0.159\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-esempio-2">

### Gain one decade above

Low-pass with $\omega_c=1$: $|H|$ at $\omega=10$.

**Solution.**

1. $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{101}}\approx0.0995\to-20.04\,\mathrm{dB}$ ($\approx-20\,\mathrm{dB}$).

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-esempio-3">

### Type of filter

$H=\dfrac{s}{s+2}$.

**Solution.**

1. $H(0)=0$, $|H(\infty)|=1$: high-pass with $\omega_c=2$.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="risposta-in-frequenza-filtri-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Saying that the 3 dB cut-off frequency is where the magnitude is half its maximum.
- **Right:** It is where the magnitude is $|H|_{max}/\sqrt2$ (half power).

</div>
