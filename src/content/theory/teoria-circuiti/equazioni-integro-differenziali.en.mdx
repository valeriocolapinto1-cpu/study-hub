<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-intuizione-1">

With Laplace there is no need to guess the particular solution or to impose the constants at the end: the initial conditions enter the transformed equation from the first step, and everything reduces to solving for $Y(s)$ and inverting.

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-intuizione-2">

The procedure is always the same, in three moves: transform (derivatives carry the initial conditions with them), solve an algebraic equation in $Y(s)$, invert with partial fractions. The denominator of $Y(s)$ contains the characteristic polynomial of the equation: its zeros are the natural modes of the response.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-teoria-1">

The equation is transformed with the derivative and integral properties, giving an _algebraic_ equation in $Y(s)$ with the initial conditions already included; solve for $Y(s)$ and invert with partial fractions.

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-teoria-2">

The advantage over the classical method (homogeneous + particular solution + constants) is that initial conditions and forcing term enter in the same step.

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-teoria-3">

**Structure of the solution.** For $a_2\ddot y+a_1\dot y+a_0y=x(t)$ one gets $Y(s)=\dfrac{X(s)}{a_2s^2+a_1s+a_0}+\dfrac{a_2\left(sy(0^-)+\dot y(0^-)\right)+a_1y(0^-)}{a_2s^2+a_1s+a_0}$: the first term is the forced response (depends only on the input), the second the free response (depends only on the initial conditions).

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-teoria-4">

**Integrals.** A term $\int_0^ty\,d\tau$ becomes $Y(s)/s$. If the integral starts at $-\infty$, as for a capacitor voltage, the value accumulated up to $0^-$ also appears: $\int_{-\infty}^ty\,d\tau\to\dfrac{Y(s)}{s}+\dfrac{1}{s}\int_{-\infty}^{0^-}y\,d\tau$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-teoria-5">

**Checks.** The result is checked by substituting $t=0$ (it must give $y(0^+)$, equal to $y(0^-)$ if there are no impulses) and by applying the initial and final value theorems directly to $Y(s)$, before inverting.

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-teoria-6">

**Comparison with the classical method.** In the classical method one finds the homogeneous solution with unknown constants, then a particular solution "guessed" from the form of the input, and only at the end imposes the initial conditions on the sum. With Laplace the three steps become one: the solution comes out with the right constants, and any input, even piecewise or impulsive, requires no guessing.

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-teoria-7">

**Systems of equations.** With several unknowns, for instance the currents of two meshes, each equation is transformed and a linear algebraic system in $Y_1(s),Y_2(s),\dots$ is obtained, solved by substitution or with Cramer's rule. The determinant of the system is the characteristic polynomial of the circuit: its zeros are the poles shared by all responses.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-esempio-1">

### Second-order zero-input response

$\ddot y+3\dot y+2y=0$, $y(0^-)=1$, $\dot y(0^-)=0$.

**Solution.**

1. $s^2Y-s+3(sY-1)+2Y=0\Rightarrow Y=\dfrac{s+3}{(s+1)(s+2)}$.
2. Partial fractions: $\dfrac{2}{s+1}-\dfrac1{s+2}$, hence $y=2e^{-t}-e^{-2t}$.
3. Check: $y(0)=1$, $\dot y(0)=-2+2=0$ ✓.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-esempio-2">

### With a forcing term

$\dot y+2y=u_{-1}(t)$, $y(0^-)=0$.

**Solution.**

1. $(s+2)Y=1/s\Rightarrow Y=\dfrac1{s(s+2)}=\tfrac12\left(\dfrac1s-\dfrac1{s+2}\right)$.
2. $y=\tfrac12(1-e^{-2t})$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-esempio-3">

### Integro-differential equation

$\dot y+y+\int_0^ty\,d\tau=u_{-1}(t)$, $y(0^-)=0$.

**Solution.**

1. $sY+Y+Y/s=1/s\Rightarrow Y=\dfrac{1}{s^2+s+1}$.
2. $(s+\tfrac12)^2+\tfrac34$: $y=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3}e^{-t/2}\sin\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}t$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-esempio-4">

### Forced plus free

$\ddot y+4\dot y+3y=6\,u_{-1}(t)$ with $y(0^-)=0$, $\dot y(0^-)=1$. Find $y(t)$ and separate free and forced responses.

**Solution.**

1. Transform: $(s^2+4s+3)Y-1=\dfrac6s\Rightarrow Y=\dfrac{6}{s(s+1)(s+3)}+\dfrac{1}{(s+1)(s+3)}$.
2. Forced: $\dfrac2s-\dfrac3{s+1}+\dfrac1{s+3}\to2-3e^{-t}+e^{-3t}$. Free: $\dfrac{1/2}{s+1}-\dfrac{1/2}{s+3}\to\tfrac12e^{-t}-\tfrac12e^{-3t}$.
3. Total: $y=2-2.5e^{-t}+0.5e^{-3t}$.
4. Check: $y(0)=2-2.5+0.5=0$ ✓, $\dot y(0)=2.5-1.5=1$ ✓, $y(\infty)=2=6/3$ ✓.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Forgetting $y(0^-)$ in the derivative.
- **Right:** $\dot y\to sY-y(0^-)$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Transforming $\ddot y$ as $s^2Y-y(0^-)$.
- **Right:** $\ddot y\to s^2Y-sy(0^-)-\dot y(0^-)$: the condition on $y$ is multiplied by $s$, and the one on the derivative is needed too.

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Inverting without checking the initial conditions.
- **Right:** Substituting $t=0$ into the solution and its derivative takes a moment and catches most errors in the residues.

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-errore-4">

- **Wrong:** Stopping at $Y(s)$ as if it were the answer.
- **Right:** The required result is $y(t)$: it must be inverted, and it must be stated for which instants it holds (usually $t\gt 0$).

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-errore-5">

- **Wrong:** Transforming $\int_0^t y\,d\tau$ as $sY$.
- **Right:** The integral divides by $s$: $\int_0^t y\,d\tau\to Y(s)/s$; it is the derivative that multiplies by $s$.

</div>
