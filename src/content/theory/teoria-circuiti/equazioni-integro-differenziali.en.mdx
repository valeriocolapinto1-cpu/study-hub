<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-intuizione-1">

With Laplace there is no need to guess the particular solution or to impose the constants at the end: the initial conditions enter the transformed equation from the first step, and everything reduces to solving for $Y(s)$ and inverting.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-teoria-1">

The equation is transformed with the derivative and integral properties, giving an _algebraic_ equation in $Y(s)$ with the initial conditions already included; solve for $Y(s)$ and invert with partial fractions.

</div>

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-teoria-2">

The advantage over the classical method (homogeneous + particular solution + constants) is that initial conditions and forcing term enter in the same step.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-esempio-1">

### Second-order zero-input response

$\ddot y+3\dot y+2y=0$, $y(0^-)=1$, $\dot y(0^-)=0$.

**Solution.**

1. $s^2Y-s+3(sY-1)+2Y=0\Rightarrow Y=\dfrac{s+3}{(s+1)(s+2)}$.
2. Partial fractions: $\dfrac{2}{s+1}-\dfrac1{s+2}$, hence $y=2e^{-t}-e^{-2t}$.
3. Check: $y(0)=1$, $\dot y(0)=-2+2=0$ ✓.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-esempio-2">

### With a forcing term

$\dot y+2y=u_{-1}(t)$, $y(0^-)=0$.

**Solution.**

1. $(s+2)Y=1/s\Rightarrow Y=\dfrac1{s(s+2)}=\tfrac12\left(\dfrac1s-\dfrac1{s+2}\right)$.
2. $y=\tfrac12(1-e^{-2t})$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-esempio-3">

### Integro-differential equation

$\dot y+y+\int_0^ty\,d\tau=u_{-1}(t)$, $y(0^-)=0$.

**Solution.**

1. $sY+Y+Y/s=1/s\Rightarrow Y=\dfrac{1}{s^2+s+1}$.
2. $(s+\tfrac12)^2+\tfrac34$: $y=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3}e^{-t/2}\sin\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}t$.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="equazioni-integro-differenziali-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Forgetting $y(0^-)$ in the derivative.
- **Right:** $\dot y\to sY-y(0^-)$.

</div>
