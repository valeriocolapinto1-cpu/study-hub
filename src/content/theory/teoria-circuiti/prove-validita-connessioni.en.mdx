<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="prove-validita-connessioni-intuizione-1">

Adding the matrices only works if, after the connection, each two-port still "sees" its ports as before. If the connection creates new current paths between the two networks, the ports stop being ports and the formulas lie: the validity test is there to find out beforehand.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="prove-validita-connessioni-teoria-1">

Adding the $Z$ (or $Y$) matrices is correct only if, after the connection, each two-port still has equal entering and leaving currents at each of its ports. Otherwise the connection alters the behaviour of the individual two-ports and the formulas do not hold.

</div>

<div data-note-id="prove-validita-connessioni-teoria-2">

**Validity test** (series-series): drive the input ports with the outputs open and check that the voltage between the terminals to be connected (which will be joined) is zero; likewise for the output. If the test fails, a 1:1 isolation transformer can be inserted at one of the ports.

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="prove-validita-connessioni-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: Why adding $Z$ requires the port condition</summary>

1. In the series-series connection the ports are in series: $v_1=v_{1a}+v_{1b}$, $v_2=v_{2a}+v_{2b}$, and the port currents are shared: $i_1=i_{1a}=i_{1b}$, $i_2=i_{2a}=i_{2b}$.
2. Adding $\mathbf v_a=Z_a\mathbf i_a$ and $\mathbf v_b=Z_b\mathbf i_b$ with $\mathbf i_a=\mathbf i_b=\mathbf i$: $\mathbf v=(Z_a+Z_b)\mathbf i$.
3. The assumption $\mathbf i_a=\mathbf i_b=\mathbf i$ holds only if, for each two-port, the current entering one terminal of a port equals the current leaving the other even after the connection: this is the condition checked by the validity test. $\square$

</details>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="prove-validita-connessioni-esempio-1">

### Test passed

Two two-ports with no common connection between their ports, connected series-series.

**Solution.**

1. Driving the ports with the others open, the voltage between the terminals to be connected is zero: the connection is legitimate, $Z=Z_a+Z_b$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="prove-validita-connessioni-esempio-2">

### Test failed

Two T networks with a common grounded terminal connected series-series.

**Solution.**

1. The direct connection short-circuits parts of the network: the current may not flow through the ports as expected; the test voltage is not zero.
2. Remedy: an ideal 1:1 transformer at one port of one of the two two-ports.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="prove-validita-connessioni-esempio-3">

### Parallel-parallel with a common ground

Two π networks (common ground) in parallel.

**Solution.**

1. They already share a terminal: the parallel-parallel connection is always legitimate and $Y=Y_a+Y_b$.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="prove-validita-connessioni-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Adding the matrices without checking the validity test.
- **Right:** Always check the port condition before using the connection formulas.

</div>

</Layer>
