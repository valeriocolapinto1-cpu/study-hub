<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-intuizione-1">

A one-port is a "black box" with two terminals: we do not care what is inside, only about the relation between the voltage across it and the current through it. To know whether the box absorbs or supplies energy, fix the reference directions once and for all and look at the sign of $v\,i$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-intuizione-2">

The sign convention is just a way of keeping the books. Choosing the passive convention says nothing about what the one-port actually does: if in the end $p=v\,i$ turns out negative, the one-port is delivering. It is like a bank statement where payments are written with a minus sign: the sign of the balance tells the fact, the convention only fixes the format.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-teoria-1">

A **port** is a pair of terminals such that, at every instant, the current entering one terminal equals the current leaving the other. A **one-port** (two-terminal element) is an element with two terminals forming a port; a **two-port** (two ports) has four terminals coupled in pairs.

</div>

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-teoria-2">

On each port we fix a reference direction for the current $i$ and one for the voltage $v$. In the **passive (load) sign convention** the current enters the terminal at the higher reference potential (the $+$ of the voltage): then $p = v\,i$ is the _absorbed_ power. In the **active (generator) sign convention** the current leaves the $+$ terminal and $p=v\,i$ is the _delivered_ power.

</div>

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-teoria-3">

The energy absorbed between $t_0$ and $t$ is $w(t_0,t)=\int_{t_0}^{t} v(\tau)\,i(\tau)\,d\tau$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-teoria-4">

**Port and KCL.** For an element with only two terminals the port condition follows from KCL: the current entering one terminal must leave from the other. In an element with more terminals, instead, a pair of terminals is a port only if the rest of the circuit guarantees it, that is, if no other current leaves the pair. This is why, when two-ports are connected, the port condition must be checked (see [validity tests for connections](../prove-validita-connessioni/)).

</div>

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-teoria-5">

**Constant and average power.** If $v$ and $i$ are constant, the energy absorbed over an interval $\Delta t$ is simply $w=v\,i\,\Delta t$. In a periodic steady state of period $T$ what counts is the average power $P=\frac1T\int_0^T v\,i\,dt$: a one-port may absorb at some instants and deliver at others, and the sign of $P$ tells who, on average, gives up energy.

</div>

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-teoria-6">

**More ports.** For a two-port, with the passive convention on both ports, the absorbed power is $p=v_1i_1+v_2i_2$: one term per port. The one-port is the case with a single port; an $n$-port has $n$ terms. Each port has its own reference directions and the convention is chosen port by port, independently of the others.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-esempio-1">

### Absorbed and delivered power

A one-port has $v = 12\,\mathrm{V}$ and $i = -2\,\mathrm{A}$ with the passive sign convention. Does it absorb or deliver power?

**Solution.**

1. $p = v\,i = 12\cdot(-2) = -24\,\mathrm{W}$ (absorbed).
2. A negative absorbed power means that the one-port _delivers_ $24\,\mathrm{W}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-esempio-2">

### A battery that charges and discharges

Across a battery $v=10\,\mathrm V$ is constant; with the passive convention $i=2\,\mathrm A$ for $0\lt t\lt 3\,\mathrm s$ and $i=-1\,\mathrm A$ for $3\lt t\lt 5\,\mathrm s$. Find the energy absorbed in the $5\,\mathrm s$ and the average power.

**Solution.**

1. First phase: $w_1=v\,i\,\Delta t=10\cdot2\cdot3=60\,\mathrm J$ absorbed (the battery charges).
2. Second phase: $w_2=10\cdot(-1)\cdot2=-20\,\mathrm J$, i.e. $20\,\mathrm J$ delivered.
3. Total $w=60-20=40\,\mathrm J$ absorbed; average power $P=40/5=8\,\mathrm W$ absorbed.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Using $p=vi$ as the absorbed power when $i$ is referenced as leaving the $+$ terminal.
- **Right:** With the active sign convention $p=vi$ is the delivered power; the absorbed power has the opposite sign.

</div>

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Inferring the convention from the numerical sign of $v$ or $i$.
- **Right:** The convention depends only on the reference directions drawn, not on the values: with negative values the convention stays the same and only the sign of $p$ changes.

</div>

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Concluding that a one-port that delivers power at some instant is active.
- **Right:** A capacitor can give back at some instants the energy stored earlier: what counts is the overall balance (passivity), not the instantaneous sign of $p$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="porta-e-bipolo-errore-4">

- **Wrong:** Adding the powers of two ports written with different conventions.
- **Right:** Before adding, bring all powers to the same convention (all absorbed or all delivered), changing sign where needed.

</div>

</Layer>
