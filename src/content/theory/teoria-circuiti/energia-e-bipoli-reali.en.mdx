<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-intuizione-1">

Ideal elements are handy but "too perfect": two different ideal batteries in parallel would drive an infinite current. Nature resolves the paradox with small resistances that the model neglects. A good model of a real component adds exactly these details, such as the internal resistance of a battery.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-teoria-1">

**Energies.** Resistor: absorbed power $p=Ri^2=v^2/R\ge0$ (always dissipated). Capacitor: energy $W_C=\tfrac12 C v^2$. Inductor: $W_L=\tfrac12 L i^2$. All three are passive for $R,C,L>0$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-teoria-2">

**Inconsistencies of ideal elements.** Ideal elements can produce situations with no solution or with impulsive values: two different voltage sources in parallel violate KVL; a current source on an open circuit (or in series with another one of different value) violates KCL; a short-circuited voltage source has an undetermined current; two capacitors at different voltages connected in parallel would require an instantaneous voltage jump.

</div>

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-teoria-3">

**Real one-ports.** A real component is described by a richer equivalent circuit: a real battery is a voltage source $E$ with an internal resistance $r$ in series ($v=E-r\,i$), or a current source $E/r$ in parallel with $r$. The inconsistency disappears once the (small but non-zero) resistances of the connections are taken into account.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-esempio-1">

### Two capacitors connected in parallel

$C_1=C_2=1\,\mathrm{F}$ with $v_1=2\,\mathrm{V}$ and $v_2=0$ are connected in parallel. Find the final voltage and the change in energy.

**Solution.**

1. Charge conservation: $Q=C_1 v_1 + C_2 v_2 = 2\,\mathrm{C}$. Final value: $v=Q/(C_1+C_2)=1\,\mathrm{V}$.
2. Initial energy: $\tfrac12\cdot1\cdot2^2=2\,\mathrm{J}$. Final: $\tfrac12\cdot 2\cdot1^2 = 1\,\mathrm{J}$.
3. One joule is lost: the ideal model has no dissipative element, but the real circuit always has a resistance (or radiation) that dissipates it.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Concluding that energy is always conserved in the ideal circuit.
- **Right:** An ideal connection can have an impulsive transient with energy loss: the result depends on a neglected resistance.

</div>
