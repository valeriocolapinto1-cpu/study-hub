<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-intuizione-1">

Ideal elements are handy but "too perfect": two different ideal batteries in parallel would drive an infinite current. Nature resolves the paradox with small resistances that the model neglects. A good model of a real component adds exactly these details, such as the internal resistance of a battery.

</div>

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-intuizione-2">

The two-capacitor paradox shows it well: whatever the resistance $R$ of the connection, the energy lost is always the same, half of the initial energy in the example below. If $R$ decreases the current grows and the transient gets shorter, but the dissipated energy does not change. The ideal model gives the right final balance; what it cannot describe is how the transfer happens.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-teoria-1">

**Energies.** Resistor: absorbed power $p=Ri^2=v^2/R\ge0$ (always dissipated). Capacitor: energy $W_C=\tfrac12 C v^2$. Inductor: $W_L=\tfrac12 L i^2$. All three are passive for $R,C,L>0$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-teoria-2">

**Inconsistencies of ideal elements.** Ideal elements can produce situations with no solution or with impulsive values: two different voltage sources in parallel violate KVL; a current source on an open circuit (or in series with another one of different value) violates KCL; a short-circuited voltage source has an undetermined current; two capacitors at different voltages connected in parallel would require an instantaneous voltage jump.

</div>

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-teoria-3">

**Real one-ports.** A real component is described by a richer equivalent circuit: a real battery is a voltage source $E$ with an internal resistance $r$ in series ($v=E-r\,i$), or a current source $E/r$ in parallel with $r$. The inconsistency disappears once the (small but non-zero) resistances of the connections are taken into account.

</div>

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-teoria-4">

**Power in the three elements.** In the resistor $p=Ri^2\ge0$ at every instant: energy goes in and does not come back. In the capacitor $p=v\,i=C\,v\,\dfrac{dv}{dt}=\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(\tfrac12Cv^2\right)$: power is the derivative of the stored energy, positive while $|v|$ grows and negative while it decreases. In the inductor, by duality, $p=\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(\tfrac12Li^2\right)$. In a periodic steady state the stored energy returns to its initial value every period, so $L$ and $C$ absorb zero average power.

</div>

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-teoria-5">

**The two battery models.** The source $E$ in series with $r$ and the source $E/r$ in parallel with $r$ have the same terminal characteristic $v=E-r\,i$, so they are equivalent for the external circuit. They are not equivalent for what happens inside: on open circuit the first dissipates nothing, the second dissipates $E^2/r$ in its resistance.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-esempio-1">

### Two capacitors connected in parallel

$C_1=C_2=1\,\mathrm{F}$ with $v_1=2\,\mathrm{V}$ and $v_2=0$ are connected in parallel. Find the final voltage and the change in energy.

**Solution.**

1. Charge conservation: $Q=C_1 v_1 + C_2 v_2 = 2\,\mathrm{C}$. Final value: $v=Q/(C_1+C_2)=1\,\mathrm{V}$.
2. Initial energy: $\tfrac12\cdot1\cdot2^2=2\,\mathrm{J}$. Final: $\tfrac12\cdot 2\cdot1^2 = 1\,\mathrm{J}$.
3. One joule is lost: the ideal model has no dissipative element, but the real circuit always has a resistance (or radiation) that dissipates it.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-esempio-2">

### Power balance of a real battery

A battery with $E=12\,\mathrm V$ and internal resistance $r=0.5\,\Omega$ feeds a load $R=5.5\,\Omega$. Find current, terminal voltage, powers and efficiency.

**Solution.**

1. $i=\dfrac{E}{r+R}=\dfrac{12}{6}=2\,\mathrm A$; $v=E-r\,i=12-1=11\,\mathrm V$.
2. Power in the load $P_R=v\,i=22\,\mathrm W$; lost in the battery $P_r=r\,i^2=0.5\cdot4=2\,\mathrm W$.
3. Delivered by the source $P_E=E\,i=24\,\mathrm W=22+2$ ✓; efficiency $\eta=22/24\approx91.7\,\%$.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Concluding that energy is always conserved in the ideal circuit.
- **Right:** An ideal connection can have an impulsive transient with energy loss: the result depends on a neglected resistance.

</div>

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Putting two equal ideal voltage sources in parallel and computing how much current each supplies.
- **Right:** With equal values KVL is satisfied, but the sharing of the current between them is undetermined: the internal resistances are needed to decide it.

</div>

<div data-note-id="energia-e-bipoli-reali-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Computing the power in a resistor as $R\,i$.
- **Right:** $R\,i$ is the voltage; the power is $p=R\,i^2=v^2/R$ (unit check: $\Omega\cdot\mathrm A^2=\mathrm W$).

</div>
