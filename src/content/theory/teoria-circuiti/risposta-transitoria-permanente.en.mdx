<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="risposta-transitoria-permanente-intuizione-1">

As soon as an input is switched on, the circuit "settles": for a while its natural modes coexist with the response to the input. In a stable system the former die out and only the latter remains, with the same shape as the input: the steady-state response.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="risposta-transitoria-permanente-teoria-1">

For a stable system and a sinusoidal input $x=A\cos(\omega_0t+\varphi)$ the **steady-state response** is $y_p=A|F(j\omega_0)|\cos(\omega_0t+\varphi+\arg F(j\omega_0))$; the **transient** is made of the natural modes (poles of $F$) and dies out.

</div>

<div data-note-id="risposta-transitoria-permanente-teoria-2">

With Laplace: $Y(s)=F(s)X(s)$; in the partial-fraction expansion the poles of $X$ give the steady state, the poles of $F$ the transient.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-transitoria-permanente-esempio-1">

### RC with a cosine input

$F=\dfrac1{s+1}$, $x=\cos t$, zero initial conditions.

**Solution.**

1. $F(j)=\dfrac{1}{1+j}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}e^{-j45^\circ}$: $y_p=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\cos(t-45^\circ)=\tfrac12\cos t+\tfrac12\sin t$.
2. $Y=\dfrac{s}{(s+1)(s^2+1)}=\dfrac{-1/2}{s+1}+\dfrac{\tfrac12s+\tfrac12}{s^2+1}$: transient $-\tfrac12e^{-t}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-transitoria-permanente-esempio-2">

### Settling time

After how long is the transient $-\tfrac12e^{-t}$ below $1\%$ of the steady-state amplitude ($\tfrac1{\sqrt2}$)?

**Solution.**

1. $\tfrac12e^{-t}<0.007\Rightarrow t>\ln(71)\approx4.3\,\mathrm s\approx4.3\tau$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="risposta-transitoria-permanente-esempio-3">

### Step input

Same $F$, $x=u_{-1}$.

**Solution.**

1. Steady state: $F(0)=1$ (constant); transient: $-e^{-t}$.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="risposta-transitoria-permanente-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Computing the steady state with $F(s)$ at $s=\omega_0$.
- **Right:** $F$ is evaluated at $s=j\omega_0$.
