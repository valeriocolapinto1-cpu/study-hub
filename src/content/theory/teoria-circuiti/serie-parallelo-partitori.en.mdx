<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-intuizione-1">

In series the current is forced through every resistor, so the "obstacles" add up; in parallel it has several paths, so the "ease" (the conductances) adds up. A divider tells how the voltage (or current) is shared: in proportion to who offers more resistance (or more conductance).

</div>

<div data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-intuizione-2">

Two limiting cases help remember the formulas. In series with a very large resistor, a small one hardly matters: $R_{eq}\approx R_{large}$. In parallel the opposite happens: the small resistor "shorts" the large one and $R_{eq}\approx R_{small}$. The parallel is always smaller than the smallest resistance, the series always larger than the largest.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-teoria-1">

**Series.** One-ports carrying the same current: $R_{eq}=\sum R_k$. **Parallel.** One-ports with the same voltage: $G_{eq}=\sum G_k$, i.e. $\dfrac1{R_{eq}}=\sum\dfrac1{R_k}$; for two resistors $R_{eq}=\dfrac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2}$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-teoria-2">

**Voltage divider** (series, total voltage $v$): $v_k=v\,\dfrac{R_k}{\sum_j R_j}$. **Current divider** (parallel, total current $i$): $i_k=i\,\dfrac{G_k}{\sum_j G_j}$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-teoria-3">

Condition for the voltage divider: the current is the same in all the resistors, i.e. no downstream load drawing current.

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: Voltage divider</summary>

1. Common current (KCL): $i=v/(R_1+R_2)$.
2. Constitutive relation of $R_2$: $v_2=R_2\,i=v\,\dfrac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}$. $\square$

</details>

<div data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-teoria-4">

**Recognising series and parallel.** Two one-ports are in series if they share a node to which nothing else is connected (same current by KCL); they are in parallel if they are connected to the same pair of nodes (same voltage by KVL). The criterion holds for any one-port, not only resistors: two voltage sources in series add up, two current sources in parallel add up.

</div>

<div data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-teoria-5">

**Loaded divider.** If a load $R_L$ is connected to the middle node of an $R_1$–$R_2$ divider, the output voltage becomes $v\,\dfrac{R_2\parallel R_L}{R_1+R_2\parallel R_L}$, always lower than the open-circuit value. The unloaded divider is a good approximation only if $R_L\gg R_2$. It is a first example of a Thévenin equivalent: open-circuit voltage $v\,R_2/(R_1+R_2)$ and resistance $R_1\parallel R_2$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-teoria-6">

**Beyond resistors.** In sinusoidal steady state the same rules hold for impedances (series: $Z_{eq}=\sum Z_k$; parallel: $Y_{eq}=\sum Y_k$). In the time domain inductors in series add like resistors, $L_{eq}=\sum L_k$, while capacitors add in parallel, $C_{eq}=\sum C_k$, like conductances.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-esempio-1">

### Series-parallel network

$R_1=6\,\Omega$ and $R_2=3\,\Omega$ in parallel, in series with $R_3=4\,\Omega$, fed by $18\,\mathrm{V}$. Find the current $i$ in $R_1$.

**Solution.**

1. $R_{12}=6\cdot3/9=2\,\Omega$, $R_{eq}=2+4=6\,\Omega$, $i_{tot}=18/6=3\,\mathrm{A}$.
2. Current divider: $i_1=3\cdot\dfrac{1/6}{1/6+1/3}=3\cdot\dfrac13=1\,\mathrm{A}$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-esempio-2">

### Loaded divider

Divider with $v=12\,\mathrm V$, $R_1=R_2=10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$. Find the output unloaded and with a load $R_L=10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$; check with the Thévenin equivalent.

**Solution.**

1. Unloaded: $v_o=12\cdot\dfrac{10}{20}=6\,\mathrm V$.
2. With the load: $R_2\parallel R_L=5\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$, $v_o=12\cdot\dfrac{5}{10+5}=4\,\mathrm V$.
3. Thévenin: $V_{th}=6\,\mathrm V$, $R_{th}=10\parallel10=5\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$; across the load $6\cdot\dfrac{10}{5+10}=4\,\mathrm V$ ✓. The load "steals" one third of the voltage.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Using the voltage divider with a load connected to the middle node without including it.
- **Right:** The load is in parallel with $R_2$: use $R_2\parallel R_L$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Treating as series two resistors that share a node to which a third branch is also connected.
- **Right:** Series requires the same current: if another branch reaches the common node, the current splits and the two resistors are not in series.

</div>

<div data-note-id="serie-parallelo-partitori-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** In the current divider writing $i_1=i\,\dfrac{R_1}{R_1+R_2}$.
- **Right:** With two branches $i_1=i\,\dfrac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}$: the larger current flows in the smaller resistance (conductances, not resistances, go in the numerator).

</div>

</Layer>
