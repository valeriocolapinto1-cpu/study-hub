<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-intuizione-1">

When a circuit connects to the outside world through several pairs of terminals, it can be treated as a box with $N$ "windows". $N$ relations between the window voltages and currents are enough to describe it, provided that every pair of terminals really is a port.

</div>

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-intuizione-2">

Thinking in ports changes the level of detail: what happens inside no longer matters, only how the quantities of one port affect those of the others. It is Thévenin's step extended to several pairs of terminals: a complicated circuit becomes a matrix.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-teoria-1">

An **$N$-port network** is a circuit with $N$ accessible pairs of terminals (ports), for each of which the current entering one terminal equals the current leaving the other. In the linear case it is described by $N$ linear relations among the $2N$ variables $v_k,i_k$: $N$ of them are chosen as independent and the others are functions of them ($Z$, $Y$, hybrid, transmission representations).

</div>

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-teoria-2">

A two-port ($N=2$) has two relations among $v_1,i_1,v_2,i_2$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-teoria-3">

**Representations.** In a linear $N$-port the $2N$ quantities are linked by $N$ equations: one chooses $N$ independent variables and expresses the other $N$ in terms of them. Choosing the currents gives the matrix $Z$, choosing the voltages $Y$, mixed choices the hybrid matrices. Not every choice is possible for every network.

</div>

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-teoria-4">

**The port condition depends on the outside.** It holds if every port is closed on a one-port (a source or a load); it can fail if terminals of different ports are connected together. This is why connections of two-ports require the [validity tests](../prove-validita-connessioni/).

</div>

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-teoria-5">

**Power.** With currents entering all ports the absorbed power is $p=\sum_kv_ki_k$, one term per port. A network of positive resistors only always absorbs $p\ge0$: it is passive.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="reti-n-porte-esempio-1">

### Counting

How many relations describe a linear 3-port network?

**Solution.**

1. 3 relations among 6 variables ($v_1,v_2,v_3,i_1,i_2,i_3$).

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="reti-n-porte-esempio-2">

### Port condition

A two-port has $i_1=2$ entering $A$ and $1$ leaving $B$. Is it a port?

**Solution.**

1. No: the entering current does not equal the leaving one; the pair does not satisfy the port condition.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="reti-n-porte-esempio-3">

### A known two-port

The ideal transformer: which variables are independent?

**Solution.**

1. $v_1=nv_2$, $i_1=-i_2/n$: $(v_2,i_2)$ can be chosen; neither $Z$ nor $Y$ exists.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="reti-n-porte-esempio-4">

### Closing a port on a load

A two-port has $Z=\begin{pmatrix}4&3\\3&5\end{pmatrix}\,\Omega$ (the T network with $1$, $2$ and $3\,\Omega$). Port 2 is closed on $R_L=2\,\Omega$: find the impedance seen from port 1.

**Solution.**

1. Load: $v_2=-2i_2$ (the load current is $-i_2$). Second equation: $3i_1+5i_2=-2i_2\Rightarrow i_2=-\tfrac37i_1$.
2. First equation: $v_1=4i_1+3i_2=\left(4-\tfrac97\right)i_1=\tfrac{19}{7}i_1$, so $Z_{in}=\tfrac{19}7\approx2.71\,\Omega$.
3. Check on the T network: $1+3\parallel(2+2)=1+\tfrac{12}{7}=\tfrac{19}{7}$ ✓. Closing a port turns the two-port into a one-port.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Treating as a port a pair of terminals that does not satisfy the port condition.
- **Right:** Check that $i_{in}=i_{out}$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Thinking that an $N$-port always has a $Z$ matrix.
- **Right:** It exists only if the currents can be chosen freely: the ideal transformer, for instance, has neither $Z$ nor $Y$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Closing a port on a load by writing $v_2=R_Li_2$.
- **Right:** With $i_2$ entering the network, the load carries $-i_2$: $v_2=-R_Li_2$.

</div>
