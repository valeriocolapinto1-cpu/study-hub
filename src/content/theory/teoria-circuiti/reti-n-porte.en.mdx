<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-intuizione-1">

When a circuit connects to the outside world through several pairs of terminals, it can be treated as a box with $N$ "windows". $N$ relations between the window voltages and currents are enough to describe it, provided that every pair of terminals really is a port.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-teoria-1">

An **$N$-port network** is a circuit with $N$ accessible pairs of terminals (ports), for each of which the current entering one terminal equals the current leaving the other. In the linear case it is described by $N$ linear relations among the $2N$ variables $v_k,i_k$: $N$ of them are chosen as independent and the others are functions of them ($Z$, $Y$, hybrid, transmission representations).

</div>

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-teoria-2">

A two-port ($N=2$) has two relations among $v_1,i_1,v_2,i_2$.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="reti-n-porte-esempio-1">

### Counting

How many relations describe a linear 3-port network?

**Solution.**

1. 3 relations among 6 variables ($v_1,v_2,v_3,i_1,i_2,i_3$).

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="reti-n-porte-esempio-2">

### Port condition

A two-port has $i_1=2$ entering $A$ and $1$ leaving $B$. Is it a port?

**Solution.**

1. No: the entering current does not equal the leaving one; the pair does not satisfy the port condition.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="reti-n-porte-esempio-3">

### A known two-port

The ideal transformer: which variables are independent?

**Solution.**

1. $v_1=nv_2$, $i_1=-i_2/n$: $(v_2,i_2)$ can be chosen; neither $Z$ nor $Y$ exists.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="reti-n-porte-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Treating as a port a pair of terminals that does not satisfy the port condition.
- **Right:** Check that $i_{in}=i_{out}$.

</div>

</Layer>
