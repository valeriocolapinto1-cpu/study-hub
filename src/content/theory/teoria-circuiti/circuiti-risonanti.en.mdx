<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-intuizione-1">

In an RLC circuit, inductor and capacitor exchange energy like a spring and a mass. At the resonant frequency their reactances cancel each other and the circuit responds at its maximum: the smaller the losses (a small resistance in the series circuit, a large one in the parallel circuit), the "sharper" and higher the resonance.

</div>

<div data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-intuizione-2">

At resonance the energy bounces between inductor and capacitor without passing through the source: when one grows the other shrinks and their sum stays constant. The source only has to replace what the resistance dissipates each cycle; this is why $Q$, which compares stored and dissipated energy, measures how "clean" the resonance is.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-teoria-1">

**Series RLC.** $Z=R+j(\omega L-1/(\omega C))$. At **resonance** $\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}$ the reactance is zero, $|Z|$ is minimum and equal to $R$. Quality factor $Q=\dfrac{\omega_0L}{R}=\dfrac{1}{\omega_0RC}$; $3\,\mathrm{dB}$ bandwidth $B=\omega_0/Q=R/L$. At resonance the voltage across $L$ and $C$ is $Q$ times the source voltage.

</div>

<div data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-teoria-2">

**Parallel RLC.** $Y=G+j(\omega C-1/(\omega L))$: at resonance the admittance is minimum and the impedance maximum ($=R$). $Q=\dfrac{R}{\omega_0L}=\omega_0RC$, $B=\omega_0/Q=1/(RC)$. It is the dual of the series case.

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: 3 dB bandwidth of the series RLC</summary>

**Statement.** The $3\,\mathrm{dB}$ bandwidth of the current is exactly $B=\omega_2-\omega_1=R/L=\omega_0/Q$.

1. $|I|/|I|_{max}=1/\sqrt2$ when $|X|=R$, i.e. $\omega L-\dfrac1{\omega C}=\pm R$.
2. Solving $\omega^2LC\mp\omega RC-1=0$: $\omega_{1,2}=\mp\dfrac{R}{2L}+\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{R}{2L}\right)^2+\dfrac1{LC}}$ (positive roots).
3. Subtracting: $\omega_2-\omega_1=\dfrac{R}{L}=\dfrac{\omega_0}{Q}$ (the two positive roots differ by exactly $R/L$). $\square$

</details>

<div data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-teoria-3">

**Energy definition of $Q$.** $Q=2\pi\dfrac{W_{st}}{W_{diss,cycle}}=\dfrac{\omega_0W_{st}}{P}$, with $W_{st}$ the stored energy (constant at resonance) and $P$ the average dissipated power. For the series circuit it gives $\omega_0L/R$, for the parallel one $R/(\omega_0L)$: the same definition explains why the two expressions depend on $R$ in opposite ways.

</div>

<div data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-teoria-4">

**Cutoff frequencies.** The $-3\,\mathrm{dB}$ angular frequencies are $\omega_{1,2}=\mp\dfrac{B}{2}+\sqrt{\dfrac{B^2}{4}+\omega_0^2}$, with $\omega_2-\omega_1=B$ and $\omega_1\omega_2=\omega_0^2$: $\omega_0$ is their geometric mean, not the arithmetic one (the two nearly coincide when $Q$ is large).

</div>

<div data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-teoria-5">

**Overvoltages and overcurrents.** In the series circuit, at resonance, the voltages across $L$ and $C$ are $Q$ times the source voltage and in phase opposition; in the parallel one, dually, the currents in $L$ and $C$ are $Q$ times the source current. With a large $Q$ these values can far exceed the components' ratings.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-esempio-1">

### Numerical series RLC

$R=1\,\Omega$, $L=1\,\mathrm H$, $C=\tfrac14\,\mathrm F$.

**Solution.**

1. $\omega_0=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1/4}}=2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, $Q=\dfrac{2\cdot1}{1}=2$, $B=\dfrac{R}{L}=1\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ ($=\omega_0/Q$ ✓).

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-esempio-2">

### Parallel RLC

$R=4\,\Omega$, $L=1\,\mathrm H$, $C=1\,\mathrm F$.

**Solution.**

1. $\omega_0=1$, $Q=\dfrac{R}{\omega_0L}=4$, $B=\dfrac{1}{RC}=\tfrac14$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-esempio-3">

### Overvoltage

Series circuit with $Q=10$ fed by $1\,\mathrm V$.

**Solution.**

1. At resonance $|V_C|=|V_L|=Q\cdot1=10\,\mathrm V$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-esempio-4">

### Series RLC: bandwidth and overvoltage

$R=10\,\Omega$, $L=10\,\mathrm{mH}$, $C=1\,\mu\mathrm F$, source of $1\,\mathrm V$ RMS. Find $\omega_0$, $Q$, $B$, the cutoff frequencies and the capacitor voltage at resonance.

**Solution.**

1. $\omega_0=\dfrac1{\sqrt{10^{-2}\cdot10^{-6}}}=10^4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ ($f_0\approx1592\,\mathrm{Hz}$); $Q=\dfrac{\omega_0L}{R}=\dfrac{10^4\cdot0.01}{10}=10$.
2. $B=R/L=1000\,\mathrm{rad/s}$; $\sqrt{500^2+10^8}=10\,012.5$, so $\omega_1=9512.5$ and $\omega_2=10\,512.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.
3. At resonance $I=1/10=0.1\,\mathrm A$ and $V_C=\dfrac{I}{\omega_0C}=\dfrac{0.1}{10^4\cdot10^{-6}}=10\,\mathrm V=Q\cdot1\,\mathrm V$ ✓.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Using $Q=\omega_0L/R$ for the parallel circuit as well.
- **Right:** In the parallel circuit $Q=R/(\omega_0L)=\omega_0RC$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Thinking that at resonance a voltage-driven parallel RLC draws maximum current.
- **Right:** In the parallel circuit at resonance the impedance is maximum: with a voltage source the current is minimum; with a current source it is the voltage that is maximum.

</div>

<div data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Computing $\omega_0$ as $1/(LC)$.
- **Right:** $\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}$; unit check: $LC$ is measured in $\mathrm s^2$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="circuiti-risonanti-errore-4">

- **Wrong:** Computing the bandwidth as $B=\omega_0Q$.
- **Right:** $B=\omega_0/Q$: the more selective the resonance (large $Q$), the narrower the band.

</div>
