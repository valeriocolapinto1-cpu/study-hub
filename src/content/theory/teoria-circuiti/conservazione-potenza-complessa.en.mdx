<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-intuizione-1">

Just as in DC no energy appears from nowhere, in sinusoidal steady state active and reactive power each "balance the books" separately. Apparent powers do not: they are magnitudes, and magnitudes of complex numbers do not add up.

</div>

<div data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-intuizione-2">

The balance is also a checking tool: after solving a circuit in sinusoidal steady state, adding up $P$ of the resistors and $Q$ of inductors and capacitors and comparing them with what the sources deliver takes a minute and catches almost every sign or arithmetic error.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-teoria-1">

In sinusoidal steady state the sum of the complex powers absorbed by all the branches (sources included, passive convention) is zero: $\sum_k\dot V_k\dot I_k^*=0$. Hence $\sum P_k=0$ and $\sum Q_k=0$ are conserved separately, while the magnitudes $|S_k|$ are not.

</div>

<div data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-teoria-2">

**Balance.** The active power delivered by the sources equals the power absorbed by the resistors; the reactive power delivered equals the reactive power absorbed by inductors and capacitors (with sign).

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: Conservation of complex power</summary>

**Statement.** $\sum_k\dot V_k\dot I_k^*=0$ (RMS values, passive convention).

1. The phasors $\dot{\mathbf V}$ satisfy KVL and $\dot{\mathbf I}$ satisfy KCL (the equations have real coefficients $0,\pm1$).
2. $\dot{\mathbf I}^*$ also satisfies KCL, because conjugation preserves equations with real coefficients.
3. By Tellegen's theorem $\dot{\mathbf V}^{\mathsf T}\dot{\mathbf I}^*=0$, i.e. $\sum_k\dot V_k\dot I_k^*=0$. Real part: $\sum P_k=0$; imaginary part: $\sum Q_k=0$. $\square$

</details>

<div data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-teoria-3">

**What is not conserved.** Apparent power is not conserved because it is a modulus: $|S_1+S_2|\le|S_1|+|S_2|$, with equality only if the two loads have the same power factor. For the same reason the power factor of several loads is not the average of the individual ones: it is computed from $P_{tot}$ and $Q_{tot}$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-teoria-4">

**Loads in parallel.** Several loads fed by the same voltage combine by adding their complex powers: $S_{tot}=\sum_kS_k$, i.e. $P_{tot}=\sum P_k$ and $Q_{tot}=\sum Q_k$. The line current then follows from $I=|S_{tot}|/V$.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-esempio-1">

### Balance on a series RL

$\dot V_g=10\,\mathrm V$ (RMS) on $R=3\,\Omega$ in series with $X_L=4\,\Omega$. Check the balance.

**Solution.**

1. $\dot I=\dfrac{10}{3+4j}=1.2-1.6j$, $|I|^2=4$.
2. Source (delivered): $S=\dot V\dot I^*=10(1.2+1.6j)=12+16j$.
3. Absorbed: $R$: $P=3\cdot4=12$; $L$: $Q=4\cdot4=16$. It balances. ✓

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-esempio-2">

### What is not conserved

In a circuit $|S_g|=20$, $|S_R|=12$, $|S_L|=16$. Are the magnitudes conserved?

**Solution.**

1. $12+16=28\ne20$: the magnitudes are not; $P$ ($12$) and $Q$ ($16$) are.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-esempio-3">

### Reactive power with a capacitor

Load $P=12$, $Q_L=16$ with a capacitor absorbing $Q_C=-16$.

**Solution.**

1. $\sum Q=16-16=0$: the source delivers only active power: full power-factor correction.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-esempio-4">

### Two loads in parallel

At an RMS voltage $V=200\,\mathrm V$ are connected a load of $P_1=1000\,\mathrm W$ with $\cos\varphi_1=0.8$ inductive and one of $P_2=600\,\mathrm W$ with $\cos\varphi_2=0.6$ capacitive. Find the total complex power, the power factor and the line current.

**Solution.**

1. $Q_1=P_1\tan\varphi_1=1000\cdot0.75=750\,\mathrm{var}$; $Q_2=-600\cdot\tfrac43=-800\,\mathrm{var}$.
2. $S_{tot}=1600-50j$, $|S_{tot}|=1600.8\,\mathrm{VA}$, $\cos\varphi=1600/1600.8=0.9995$.
3. $I=1600.8/200=8.0\,\mathrm A$. Adding the apparent powers ($1250+1000=2250\,\mathrm{VA}$) would give $11.25\,\mathrm A$: wrong.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Adding the apparent powers of the branches.
- **Right:** Add the complex powers (or $P$ and $Q$ separately).

</div>

<div data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Computing the power factor of several loads as the average of the individual ones.
- **Right:** It is computed from the totals: $\cos\varphi=P_{tot}/\sqrt{P_{tot}^2+Q_{tot}^2}$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Leaving controlled sources out of the balance.
- **Right:** A controlled source is a branch like the others: its complex power enters the sum, and it may be delivered.

</div>

<div data-note-id="conservazione-potenza-complessa-errore-4">

- **Wrong:** Computing the line current by adding the magnitudes of the load currents.
- **Right:** Phasors are added (KCL), not magnitudes: in the example the line carries $8\,\mathrm A$, not $6.25+5=11.25\,\mathrm A$.

</div>

</Layer>
