<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="stabilita-poli-intuizione-1">

A circuit left to itself after a disturbance: does it return to rest, keep oscillating or "blow up"? The answer lies entirely in where the poles are: to the left of the imaginary axis the modes die out, on the axis they survive, to the right they grow without bound.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="stabilita-poli-teoria-1">

A linear system is **asymptotically stable** if the zero-input response tends to zero for every initial condition; necessary and sufficient condition: all the poles of $H(s)$ (without cancellations) have negative real part. It is **marginally stable** with simple poles on the imaginary axis (and the others with negative real part); it is **unstable** with a pole with positive real part or multiple poles on the imaginary axis.

</div>

<div data-note-id="stabilita-poli-teoria-2">

**Criteria for polynomials.** Degree 2: $s^2+a_1s+a_0$ is stable if $a_1,a_0>0$. Degree 3: $s^3+a_2s^2+a_1s+a_0$ is stable if $a_2,a_1,a_0>0$ and $a_2a_1>a_0$.

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="stabilita-poli-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: Stability condition: poles in the open left half-plane</summary>

1. Expanding in partial fractions, every pole $p$ of multiplicity $m$ gives modes $t^ke^{pt}$ with $0\le k<m$.
2. If $\mathrm{Re}\,p<0$: $|t^ke^{pt}|=t^ke^{\mathrm{Re}(p)t}\to0$ (the exponential beats the power).
3. If all the poles have $\mathrm{Re}<0$, every mode, and hence the zero-input response, tends to zero.
4. If a pole has $\mathrm{Re}\,p>0$ the mode diverges; if $\mathrm{Re}\,p=0$ the mode does not tend to zero ($|e^{j\omega t}|=1$), and if the multiplicity is $\ge2$ the term $t\,e^{j\omega t}$ appears, unbounded. $\square$

</details>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="stabilita-poli-esempio-1">

### Second order with a parameter

$D(s)=s^2+(k-3)s+2$.

**Solution.**

1. $k-3>0$ is needed: stable for $k>3$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="stabilita-poli-esempio-2">

### Third order

$D(s)=s^3+2s^2+3s+k$.

**Solution.**

1. $k>0$ and $2\cdot3>k$: stable for $0<k<6$.
2. For $k=6$: poles on the imaginary axis ($s=\pm j\sqrt3$): marginal.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="stabilita-poli-esempio-3">

### Pole at $+1$

$H=\dfrac{1}{s-1}$.

**Solution.**

1. Pole at $s=1$: $h=e^{t}$ diverges, unstable.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="stabilita-poli-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Saying that a pole on the imaginary axis makes the system stable.
- **Right:** At most marginal: the response does not decay and may become unbounded (multiple pole or resonant input).

</div>
