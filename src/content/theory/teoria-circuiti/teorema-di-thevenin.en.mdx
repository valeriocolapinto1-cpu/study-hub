<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-thevenin-intuizione-1">

Seen from two terminals, a linear circuit, however complicated, behaves like a real battery: an "open-circuit" voltage and an internal resistance. All the internal complexity boils down to two numbers, which is why Thévenin is the heart of the analysis exercise.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-thevenin-teoria-1">

**Thévenin's theorem.** A linear one-port (not coupled with the load) is equivalent, as far as the connected load is concerned, to an ideal voltage source $V_{th}$ in series with an impedance $Z_{th}$: $V_{th}$ is the open-circuit voltage; $Z_{th}$ is the impedance seen at the terminals with the _independent_ sources switched off (voltage → short, current → open), or $V_{th}/I_{sc}$. With controlled sources, $Z_{th}$ is found with a test source ($V/I$), leaving the controlled sources in place.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-thevenin-teoria-2">

**When it does not apply.** The theorem does not hold if the one-port contains nonlinear elements, or if there is coupling between the one-port and the load (cross controls). For an ideal current source $Z_{th}=\infty$: only Norton exists.

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="teorema-di-thevenin-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: Thévenin's theorem</summary>

**Statement.** For a linear one-port $\mathcal N$ with terminals $A$, $B$: $v=V_{th}-Z_{th}\,i$ (with $i$ entering the load).

1. Connect any load to the one-port; by the substitution theorem it is replaced by a current source $i$ (the current leaving the one-port).
2. The circuit is linear: by superposition the terminal voltage $v$ is the sum of two contributions.
3. First contribution: internal sources on and current source $i$ off (open circuit): it is the open-circuit voltage $V_{th}$.
4. Second contribution: internal independent sources off and only the source $i$ on: by linearity it is proportional to $i$, $-Z_{th}\,i$, with $Z_{th}$ the impedance seen from the terminals with independent sources off.
5. $v=V_{th}-Z_{th}i$ is the characteristic of a source $V_{th}$ in series with $Z_{th}$. $\square$

</details>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-thevenin-esempio-1">

### Divider

$12\,\mathrm V$ in series with $R_1=2\,\Omega$, then $R_2=4\,\Omega$ to ground; port across $R_2$.

**Solution.**

1. $V_{th}=12\cdot\dfrac46=8\,\mathrm V$, $R_{th}=2\parallel4=\dfrac43\,\Omega$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-thevenin-esempio-2">

### With a controlled source

$V_g=4\,\mathrm V$, $R_1=2\,\Omega$ between $a$ and $o$; a VCCS draws the current $i=\tfrac12v_o$ from $o$ to ground. Port at $o$.

**Solution.**

1. The VCCS behaves as a $2\,\Omega$ resistor to ground and _is not switched off_.
2. $V_{th}=4\cdot\dfrac{2}{2+2}=2\,\mathrm V$.
3. Test source of $1\,\mathrm A$ into $o$ (with $V_g$ shorted): $v_o=1/(\tfrac12+\tfrac12)=1\,\mathrm V$, $R_{th}=1\,\Omega$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-thevenin-esempio-3">

### Check with a short circuit

Same example: short-circuit current.

**Solution.**

1. With $o$ shorted ($v_o=0$) the VCCS draws nothing: $I_{sc}=4/2=2\,\mathrm A$.
2. $V_{th}/R_{th}=2/1=2$ ✓.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-thevenin-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Switching off the controlled sources as well to find $R_{th}$.
- **Right:** Only the independent sources are switched off; the controlled ones stay and the test source is used.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-thevenin-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Applying Thévenin to a nonlinear one-port.
- **Right:** Linearity is required.

</div>

</Layer>
