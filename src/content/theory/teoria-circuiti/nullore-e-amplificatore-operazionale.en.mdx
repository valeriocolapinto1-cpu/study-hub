<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="nullore-e-amplificatore-operazionale-intuizione-1">

An operational amplifier with enormous gain, closed in a feedback loop, adjusts itself until the difference between its inputs is practically zero. The nullor captures this behaviour exactly: at the input "no voltage and no current", at the output "whatever it takes" to satisfy that constraint.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="nullore-e-amplificatore-operazionale-teoria-1">

The **nullator** is a one-port with $v=0$ and $i=0$ at the same time. The **norator** is a one-port with _arbitrary_ voltage and current. A **nullor** is the two-port formed by the nullator (port 1) and the norator (port 2): it imposes $v_1=0$, $i_1=0$ and leaves $v_2$, $i_2$ free. Exercises usually use the unbalanced version (one common terminal).

</div>

<div data-note-id="nullore-e-amplificatore-operazionale-teoria-2">

**From the real component to the ideal one.** In its linear region the real operational amplifier can be modelled with an input resistance $R_i$ ($10^5$–$10^{12}\,\Omega$), an output resistance $R_o$ ($5$–$50\,\Omega$) and a controlled source $v_o=A\,v_d$ with $v_d=v_2-v_1$ and $A$ between $10^5$ and $10^8$. It has three regions: linear and two saturation regions ($\pm V_{cc}$). In the ideal limit $A\to\infty$, $R_i\to\infty$, $R_o=0$ we get $v_d=0$ and zero input currents: it is a nullor.

</div>

<div data-note-id="nullore-e-amplificatore-operazionale-teoria-3">

**Two typical configurations with the nullor.** (a) _Voltage source:_ the current $I_g$ flowing through $R$ produces $V=R\,I_g$; the driving source has zero voltage and delivers no power (the power comes from the nullor). (b) _Current source:_ the voltage $V_g$ is copied across $R$ and gives $I=V_g/R$; the driving source has zero current.

</div>

<div data-note-id="nullore-e-amplificatore-operazionale-teoria-4">

| Circuit              | Output                                                                             |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Inverting            | $v_o=-\dfrac{R_2}{R_1}V_g$                                                         |
| Inverting summer     | $v_o=-R_0\left(\dfrac{V_{g1}}{R_1}+\dfrac{V_{g2}}{R_2}+\dfrac{V_{g3}}{R_3}\right)$ |
| Non-inverting        | $v_o=\left(1+\dfrac{R_2}{R_1}\right)V_g$                                           |
| Voltage follower     | $v_o=V_g$ (theoretically infinite input impedance)                                 |
| Difference amplifier | $v_o=\dfrac{R_2}{R_1}(V_{g2}-V_{g1})$                                              |

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="nullore-e-amplificatore-operazionale-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: Gain of the inverting amplifier, from the finite-$A$ model to the nullor</summary>

**Statement.** With the linear model ($R_i\to\infty$, $R_o=0$, gain $A$) the gain is $v_o/V_g=-\dfrac{R_2}{R_1}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1+(1+R_2/R_1)/A}$, which tends to $-R_2/R_1$ as $A\to\infty$.

1. Non-inverting input grounded: $v_d=v_+-v_-=-v_-$, hence $v_o=A\,v_d=-A\,v_-$ and $v_-=-v_o/A$.
2. KCL at the inverting node (zero input current): $\dfrac{V_g-v_-}{R_1}=\dfrac{v_--v_o}{R_2}$.
3. Substitute $v_-$: $\dfrac{V_g+v_o/A}{R_1}=\dfrac{-v_o/A-v_o}{R_2}$, i.e. $R_2V_g+\dfrac{R_2}{A}v_o=-\dfrac{R_1}{A}v_o-R_1v_o$.
4. Collect $v_o$: $R_2V_g=-v_o\left(R_1+\dfrac{R_1+R_2}{A}\right)$, which gives the formula in the statement.
5. As $A\to\infty$: $v_d=-v_-=v_o/A\to0$ (nullator) and $v_o=-(R_2/R_1)V_g$. $\square$

</details>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="nullore-e-amplificatore-operazionale-esempio-1">

### Real versus ideal

Inverting amplifier with $R_2/R_1=10$ and $A=10^5$: compare the real gain with the ideal one.

**Solution.**

1. Ideal: $-10$.
2. Real: $-10/(1+11/10^5)=-10/1.00011\approx-9.9989$.
3. Relative difference $\approx0.011\%$: the idealisation is excellent.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="nullore-e-amplificatore-operazionale-esempio-2">

### Nullor as a voltage source

A nullor has a current source $I_g=2\,\mathrm{A}$ at the nullator, in series with $R=3\,\Omega$ towards the norator: what is the imposed voltage?

**Solution.**

1. $I_g$ flows through $R$ (the nullator draws no current): $V=R\,I_g=3\cdot2=6\,\mathrm{V}$.
2. The voltage across the nullator is zero: the driving current source delivers no power.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="nullore-e-amplificatore-operazionale-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Saying that the input current is zero "because the input voltage is zero", as for a short circuit.
- **Right:** In the nullator voltage _and_ current are both zero: the current is zero because $R_i\to\infty$, the voltage because $A\to\infty$ with feedback.

</div>

</Layer>
