<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-intuizione-1">

Norton tells the same story as Thévenin from the point of view of current: instead of a battery with a series resistance, a current source with a parallel resistance. You go from one to the other with a simple source transformation.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-intuizione-2">

Thévenin and Norton are the same box described in two ways: from outside they cannot be told apart. One picks whichever makes the algebra easier: Norton when the load is in parallel with other elements or nodal analysis is used, Thévenin when the load is in series or mesh analysis is used.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-teoria-1">

**Norton's theorem.** A linear one-port is equivalent to an ideal current source $I_N$ in parallel with an impedance $Z_N$: $I_N$ is the short-circuit current of the port; $Z_N=Z_{th}$ (independent sources off). Link: $I_N=V_{th}/Z_{th}$ (source transformation).

</div>

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-teoria-2">

It exists even when Thévenin does not (for $Z_{th}=0$ Norton does not exist).

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: Norton's theorem</summary>

1. Replace the load with a voltage source $v$ (substitution).
2. By superposition: $i=I_{sc}-Y\,v$, where $I_{sc}$ is the current with $v=0$ (port shorted) and $Y=1/Z_N$ is the admittance seen with the independent sources off.
3. This is the characteristic of a current source $I_N=I_{sc}$ in parallel with $Z_N$. Comparing with Thévenin, $I_N=V_{th}/Z_{th}$ and $Z_N=Z_{th}$. $\square$

</details>

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-teoria-3">

**Source transformation.** $V_{th}=Z_{th}I_N$ and $Z_N=Z_{th}$: a voltage source with a series impedance is equivalent to a current source $V_{th}/Z_{th}$ with the same impedance in parallel. The transformation can also be applied to internal pieces of a circuit, to simplify it one step at a time.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-teoria-4">

**When they exist.** Norton requires $Z_{th}\neq0$ (an ideal voltage source has no Norton equivalent); Thévenin requires a finite $Z_N$ (an ideal current source has no Thévenin equivalent). When both exist they describe the same terminal characteristic, $i=I_N-v/Z_N$.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-teoria-5">

**Only externally equivalent.** The two equivalents give the same voltages and currents at the load, not the same internal power: on open circuit the Thévenin model dissipates nothing, the Norton model dissipates $I_N^2R_N$. For the power dissipated inside the one-port one must go back to the real circuit.

</div>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-esempio-1">

### Source transformation

$V_{th}=8\,\mathrm V$, $R_{th}=4\,\Omega$.

**Solution.**

1. $I_N=8/4=2\,\mathrm A$, $R_N=4\,\Omega$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-esempio-2">

### Divider: Norton

$12\,\mathrm V$, $2\,\Omega$, $4\,\Omega$ (port across the $4\,\Omega$).

**Solution.**

1. $I_N=\dfrac{V_{th}}{R_{th}}=\dfrac{8}{4/3}=6\,\mathrm A$.
2. Check: with the port shorted the current is $12/2=6$ ✓.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-esempio-3">

### Load on the Norton equivalent

$I_N=6$, $R_N=\tfrac43$, load $R_L=4$.

**Solution.**

1. Current divider: $i_L=6\cdot\dfrac{4/3}{4/3+4}=6\cdot\tfrac14=1.5\,\mathrm A$, $v_L=6\,\mathrm V$; with Thévenin: $8\cdot\dfrac{4}{4/3+4}=6$ ✓.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-esempio-4">

### Two sources in parallel

Between node $A$ and ground there are: $10\,\mathrm V$ in series with $2\,\Omega$, $6\,\mathrm V$ in series with $3\,\Omega$ and a load $R_L=4\,\Omega$. Find the load voltage with Norton.

**Solution.**

1. Transformation: $10/2=5\,\mathrm A$ with $2\,\Omega$ and $6/3=2\,\mathrm A$ with $3\,\Omega$, all in parallel.
2. $I_N=5+2=7\,\mathrm A$, $R_N=2\parallel3=1.2\,\Omega$; with the load $v=7\cdot(1.2\parallel4)=7\cdot\dfrac{4.8}{5.2}=6.46\,\mathrm V$.
3. Check with nodal analysis: $\dfrac{v-10}{2}+\dfrac{v-6}{3}+\dfrac v4=0\Rightarrow13v=84$, $v=6.46\,\mathrm V$ ✓; load current $1.62\,\mathrm A$.

</div>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Computing $I_N$ with the port open.
- **Right:** $I_N$ is the current with the port short-circuited.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-errore-2">

- **Wrong:** Putting $R_N$ in series with the current source.
- **Right:** In the Norton equivalent the impedance is in parallel: in series with an ideal current source it would have no effect on the load.

</div>

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-errore-3">

- **Wrong:** Getting the direction of $I_N$ wrong.
- **Right:** $I_N$ has the direction of the short-circuit current, which leaves the positive terminal and flows through the short: with that direction the source pushes it into the load.

</div>
