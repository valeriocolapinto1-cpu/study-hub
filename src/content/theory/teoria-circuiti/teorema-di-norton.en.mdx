<Layer kind="intuizione">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-intuizione-1">

Norton tells the same story as Thévenin from the point of view of current: instead of a battery with a series resistance, a current source with a parallel resistance. You go from one to the other with a simple source transformation.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="formalismo">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-teoria-1">

**Norton's theorem.** A linear one-port is equivalent to an ideal current source $I_N$ in parallel with an impedance $Z_N$: $I_N$ is the short-circuit current of the port; $Z_N=Z_{th}$ (independent sources off). Link: $I_N=V_{th}/Z_{th}$ (source transformation).

</div>

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-teoria-2">

It exists even when Thévenin does not (for $Z_{th}=0$ Norton does not exist).

</div>

<details class="proof" data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-dimostrazione-1">
<summary>Proof: Norton's theorem</summary>

1. Replace the load with a voltage source $v$ (substitution).
2. By superposition: $i=I_{sc}-Y\,v$, where $I_{sc}$ is the current with $v=0$ (port shorted) and $Y=1/Z_N$ is the admittance seen with the independent sources off.
3. This is the characteristic of a current source $I_N=I_{sc}$ in parallel with $Z_N$. Comparing with Thévenin, $I_N=V_{th}/Z_{th}$ and $Z_N=Z_{th}$. $\square$

</details>

<Layer kind="esempio">

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-esempio-1">

### Source transformation

$V_{th}=8\,\mathrm V$, $R_{th}=4\,\Omega$.

**Solution.**

1. $I_N=8/4=2\,\mathrm A$, $R_N=4\,\Omega$.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-esempio-2">

### Divider: Norton

$12\,\mathrm V$, $2\,\Omega$, $4\,\Omega$ (port across the $4\,\Omega$).

**Solution.**

1. $I_N=\dfrac{V_{th}}{R_{th}}=\dfrac{8}{4/3}=6\,\mathrm A$.
2. Check: with the port shorted the current is $12/2=6$ ✓.

</div>

<div class="worked" data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-esempio-3">

### Load on the Norton equivalent

$I_N=6$, $R_N=\tfrac43$, load $R_L=4$.

**Solution.**

1. Current divider: $i_L=6\cdot\dfrac{4/3}{4/3+4}=6\cdot\tfrac14=1.5\,\mathrm A$, $v_L=6\,\mathrm V$; with Thévenin: $8\cdot\dfrac{4}{4/3+4}=6$ ✓.

</div>

</Layer>

<Layer kind="errori">

<div data-note-id="teorema-di-norton-errore-1">

- **Wrong:** Computing $I_N$ with the port open.
- **Right:** $I_N$ is the current with the port short-circuited.

</div>
